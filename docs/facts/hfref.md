# Fact sheet: Heart failure with reduced ejection fraction, medical management

Source: Gauer RL, Rifaat A, Foulkrod AM. Heart Failure With Reduced Ejection
Fraction: Medical Management. Am Fam Physician. 2025;112(2):131-145.
Uploaded as PDF, 2026-09-13.

Status: draft for review. Nothing here has become a question yet.

How to read this: each fact is one standalone sentence taken from the article,
grouped into topics that could each become a planet. Tags in brackets. A star
marks facts that drive a decision at the point of care. Unstarred facts are
context, distractor material, or explanation material.

This article is almost entirely management, and it is dense. Three topics
came out of it, and they are deliberately cut by the question the physician
is asking rather than by the article's headings: "what do I prescribe and
how fast", "is this patient wet and what do I do about it", and "the drugs
are on board and something is going wrong".

## Topic A. The four pillars: starting and titrating GDMT (20 facts)

Frame: a new or worsening HFrEF diagnosis in front of you. What goes on, in
what order, how fast, and what you never stop.
Tags: gdmt, arni, ace-inhibitor, beta-blocker, mra, sglt2, sequencing,
titration, target-dose

1. HFrEF is defined by a left ventricular ejection fraction of 40% or less;
   mildly reduced is 41% to 49%, preserved is 50% or more, and improved is
   initially 40% or less rising above 40%. [definition]
2. ★ Guideline-directed medical therapy (GDMT) is four drug classes:
   renin-angiotensin system or neprilysin inhibitors, beta blockers,
   mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists, and SGLT-2 inhibitors. [gdmt]
3. ★ All four together cut all-cause mortality by 61% versus placebo (hazard
   ratio 0.39), which for a 50-year-old is about 7.9 extra years of life.
   [gdmt, mortality]
4. GDMT is indicated for any symptomatic HFrEF: AHA/ACC stage C or D, NYHA
   class II to IV. [gdmt, staging]
5. ★ Guidelines call for titration to target doses within 6 to 12 weeks of
   diagnosis (SORT A); reaching targets within 6 weeks reduces 180-day
   mortality or readmission. [titration, sort]
6. Titration steps happen every 1 to 2 weeks for beta blockers and
   RAS/neprilysin inhibitors, every 4 weeks for mineralocorticoid receptor
   antagonists; SGLT-2 inhibitors are fixed dose. [titration]
7. ACE inhibitors were the first class shown to cut all-cause mortality, by
   20% to 30%; the NNT to prevent one death is 50 at 6 months and 63 at 12.
   [ace-inhibitor]
8. ARBs are for patients who cannot take an ACE inhibitor (cough,
   angioedema) and carry less mortality benefit. [arb]
9. ★ Sacubitril-valsartan is the preferred first-line agent over an ACE
   inhibitor or ARB: versus enalapril, absolute risk reduction 3.2% for
   cardiovascular death (NNT 32), 2.8% for all-cause mortality (NNT 36), and
   21% fewer heart failure readmissions over 27 months. [arni]
10. ★ Switching from an ACE inhibitor to sacubitril-valsartan needs a 36-hour
    washout to avoid angioedema; switching from an ARB needs none. [arni,
    safety]
11. Sacubitril-valsartan is best tolerated while the patient is still
    congested and can be started during a heart failure admission. [arni]
12. Sacubitril-valsartan raises BNP; follow NT-proBNP instead, which falls
    with treatment. [arni, monitoring]
13. Patients who cannot tolerate any RAS/neprilysin inhibitor (adverse
    effects, hyperkalemia) should be started on isosorbide dinitrate and
    hydralazine. [hydralazine-nitrate]
14. ★ Beta blockers with proven benefit are bisoprolol, carvedilol and
    metoprolol succinate; mortality odds ratio 0.69 versus placebo, and the
    benefit is dose dependent. [beta-blocker]
15. ★ Start beta blockers once the patient is adequately diuresed; patients
    already on one continue it through a decompensation, and if it was held
    in hospital it is restarted at discharge to avoid higher death and
    readmission rates. [beta-blocker, decompensation]
16. Withhold beta blockers in new-onset heart failure with NYHA class IV
    symptoms until the patient is stabilised; transient worsening during
    titration is expected and therapy continues. [beta-blocker]
17. ★ Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists cut mortality 15% to 30% and
    hospitalisation 15% to 40%; eplerenone causes fewer endocrine adverse
    effects (gynecomastia, erectile dysfunction) than spironolactone. [mra]
18. ★ SGLT-2 inhibitors benefit HFrEF regardless of diabetes: dapagliflozin
    cut death or worsening heart failure by 26% (NNT 21), empagliflozin cut
    cardiovascular death or hospitalisation by 25% (NNT 19) (SORT A).
    [sglt2, sort]
19. ★ There is no consensus on sequencing; a practical order for patients
    with low blood pressure is beta blocker and SGLT-2 inhibitor first, then
    a RAS/neprilysin inhibitor after 1 to 2 weeks, then a mineralocorticoid
    receptor antagonist after another 1 to 2 weeks. [sequencing,
    hypotension]
20. ★ GDMT continues indefinitely, even after ejection fraction improves or
    normalises, to prevent recurrence. [gdmt, improved-ef]

Also from this section, kept as context: an exercise-based cardiac
rehabilitation programme is recommended to reduce admissions and improve
quality of life; heart failure prevalence is 2.4% of adults and over 10%
above age 70; after a hospitalisation, one-year mortality is 29% and one-year
readmission 53%.

## Topic B. Congestion: judging volume and using diuretics (18 facts)

Frame: is this patient wet, how do I know, and how do I dry them out.
Tags: congestion, volume-assessment, pocus, natriuretic-peptide,
loop-diuretic, furosemide, diuretic-resistance, dry-weight, creatinine

1. Heart failure symptoms are driven by extracellular fluid accumulation and
   raised filling pressures; the goals of diuresis are relieving pulmonary
   oedema and reaching an ideal dry weight. [congestion]
2. ★ Clinical assessment of euvolemia is unreliable and there is no single
   diagnostic standard for pulmonary congestion. [volume-assessment]
3. Bedside signs are weak: bilateral leg oedema has 94% sensitivity but 10%
   specificity; rales 13% sensitivity and 90% specificity; a third heart
   sound 73% and 42%. [volume-assessment, accuracy]
4. Jugular venous pressure over 8 cm has the best likelihood ratio of the
   bedside signs (LR+ 2.18) but is hard to assess in obese patients.
   [volume-assessment]
5. ★ Point-of-care ultrasound of the inferior vena cava (size and
   collapsibility) and lung B-lines is more accurate for acute heart failure
   than chest radiography or natriuretic peptides. [pocus]
6. IVC under 2.1 cm with more than 50% collapse on sniff means normal right
   atrial pressure; over 2.1 cm with under 50% collapse means it is raised.
   [pocus]
7. More than three B-lines in more than two bilateral intercostal spaces is
   diagnostic of interstitial oedema. [pocus]
8. ★ Diffuse B-lines plus BNP over 100 or NT-proBNP over 900 has 88%
   sensitivity and 90% specificity (LR+ 8.8) for cardiogenic pulmonary
   oedema; lung ultrasound found 15 more cases per 100 dyspnoeic patients
   than chest radiography. [pocus, natriuretic-peptide]
9. ★ A BNP under 100 or NT-proBNP under 300 is excellent at excluding heart
   failure (negative likelihood ratios 0.08 and 0.02). [natriuretic-peptide]
10. Natriuretic peptides also rise with acute MI, pulmonary embolism,
    myocarditis, tachyarrhythmias, age, stroke, kidney dysfunction,
    cirrhosis, COPD, infection and anaemia, and run low in obesity.
    [natriuretic-peptide]
11. Adequate urinary sodium, a falling natriuretic peptide and
    haemoconcentration each indicate effective diuresis, but none alone
    predicts full decongestion. [monitoring-diuresis]
12. ★ Loop diuretics are first line for congestion, and furosemide is
    preferred (SORT C); they improve symptoms and readmissions but not
    mortality. [loop-diuretic, sort]
13. ★ Outpatient starting dose of furosemide is 20 to 40 mg orally once
    daily, higher in advanced heart failure and kidney disease. [furosemide,
    dose]
14. ★ Inpatient diuretics are given intravenously at twice the previous oral
    dose, or 20 to 40 mg for diuretic-naive patients. [furosemide, dose]
15. Torsemide and bumetanide have greater bioavailability than furosemide
    and are alternatives; furosemide's intravenous to oral ratio is 1:2, the
    others 1:1. [loop-diuretic]
16. ★ When high-dose loop diuretics fail, add a thiazide, metolazone or
    acetazolamide, and watch kidney function and electrolytes closely.
    [diuretic-resistance]
17. ★ A mild transient creatinine rise of 0.3 to 0.5 mg/dL during effective
    diuresis is associated with better outcomes; a doubling or a rise over 1
    mg/dL should prompt a search for another cause, especially if the
    patient is still congested with poor urine output. [creatinine]
18. ★ Maintain the lowest effective diuretic dose; patients self-titrate
    furosemide 20 to 40 mg for worsening symptoms or a weight gain over 3 lb
    (1.4 kg) in a day or 5 lb (2.3 kg) in a week. [dry-weight,
    self-titration]

## Topic C. When the pillars aren't enough: add-ons, iron, devices, and trouble (24 facts)

Frame: the patient is on GDMT and either still symptomatic or not tolerating
it. Secondary drugs, iron, devices, and the three common adverse effects.
Tags: digoxin, hydralazine-nitrate, ivabradine, vericiguat, iron, icd, crt,
ablation, hypotension, hyperkalemia, kidney, worsening-hf, advanced-hf,
palliative

Secondary drugs

1. ★ Digoxin added to GDMT reduces hospitalisation with no mortality effect;
   consider it for atrial fibrillation with poor rate control on maximal
   beta blocker, or persistent symptoms in sinus rhythm despite GDMT.
   [digoxin]
2. Digoxin is dosed 0.125 to 0.25 mg daily to a serum level of 0.5 to 0.9
   ng/mL, and avoided in sinus or AV block without a pacemaker. [digoxin]
3. ★ Isosorbide dinitrate with hydralazine is added to RAS/neprilysin
   inhibition in self-identified Black patients with persistent symptoms,
   and used instead of it in patients who cannot tolerate that class.
   [hydralazine-nitrate]
4. ★ Ivabradine is for symptomatic patients in sinus rhythm with a heart
   rate of 70 or more on a maximally tolerated beta blocker; it reduces
   admissions and cardiovascular mortality. [ivabradine]
5. Ivabradine is titrated at 2 weeks to a target heart rate of 50 to 60;
   adverse effects are atrial fibrillation, symptomatic bradycardia and
   visual disturbance. [ivabradine]
6. ★ Vericiguat, a soluble guanylate cyclase stimulator, is reserved for
   higher-risk patients: recent hospitalisation for worsening heart failure
   or outpatient intravenous diuretics. Avoid with long-acting nitrates or
   PDE-5 inhibitors. [vericiguat]

Iron

7. Iron deficiency affects 40% to 60% of heart failure patients with or
   without anaemia, defined as ferritin under 100, or 100 to 300 with
   transferrin saturation under 20%. [iron]
8. ★ Intravenous iron improves functional status, quality of life and
   6-minute walk distance, with no effect on all-cause mortality (SORT B);
   earlier trials showed 22% to 25% fewer heart failure hospitalisations, a
   2025 trial a nonsignificant reduction. [iron, sort]
9. Guidelines give intravenous iron a class IIa recommendation for iron
   deficiency, class I with symptomatic anaemia; oral iron is not the
   recommended route. [iron]

Devices

10. ★ Optimise GDMT before recommending an ICD or cardiac resynchronisation;
    a common class I ICD indication is ischaemic HFrEF with ejection
    fraction 35% or less despite 3 months of optimised GDMT. [icd]
11. Cardiac resynchronisation therapy is for selected patients with QRS
    duration over 150 ms. [crt]
12. Wearable defibrillators are recommended by ESC for selected patients at
    risk of sudden death who are not suitable for an implanted device.
    [wearable-defibrillator]
13. ★ Catheter ablation should be strongly considered in selected patients
    with HFrEF and atrial fibrillation; it reduces heart failure events and
    improves ejection fraction compared with conventional therapy.
    [ablation, atrial-fibrillation]

Adverse effects and deterioration

14. Hypotension affects 10% to 15% of heart failure patients; systolic under
    90 mm Hg counts as low, and GDMT gives the same benefit at low blood
    pressure as at high. [hypotension]
15. ★ Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists and SGLT-2 inhibitors barely
    lower blood pressure; carvedilol lowers it most of the beta blockers,
    yet patients with the lowest pressures on carvedilol gained the most
    survival. [hypotension, beta-blocker]
16. ★ For symptomatic hypotension, first look for non-cardiac causes and
    other blood-pressure-lowering drugs (calcium channel blockers, centrally
    acting agents, alpha blockers, nitrates), reassess the need for
    diuresis, then try retiming doses through the day. [hypotension]
17. ★ When a GDMT dose must be cut for hypotension, reduce the RAS/neprilysin
    inhibitor first, then the beta blocker, then the mineralocorticoid
    receptor antagonist; midodrine has been used off label but is linked to
    worse outcomes. [hypotension, sequencing]
18. ★ Stop a mineralocorticoid receptor antagonist when potassium exceeds 6.0
    mEq/L, reduce the dose at 5.6 to 5.9, and resume at a lower dose once
    below 5.0; adding an SGLT-2 inhibitor is prioritised to mitigate
    hyperkalemia. [hyperkalemia, mra, sglt2]
19. Mineralocorticoid receptor antagonists are contraindicated when
    potassium cannot be kept under 5.5 mEq/L, and at eGFR under 30 for
    spironolactone, 30 or less for eplerenone, 25 or less for finerenone.
    [hyperkalemia, mra, kidney]
20. Finerenone is recommended for heart failure with diabetes and stage 3 or
    4 CKD with severe albuminuria, to reduce hospitalisation and slow CKD.
    [finerenone, kidney]
21. ★ A drop in eGFR of 30% or less in a clinically stable patient is not a
    reason to stop GDMT; RAS/neprilysin inhibitors and SGLT-2 inhibitors
    both cause an early dip and then protect kidney function long term.
    [kidney, gdmt]
22. SGLT-2 inhibitors can be continued in stage 4 CKD (eGFR 15 to 29) but
    not started below an eGFR of 20 to 25 depending on the agent; they
    carry up to a threefold risk of genital infection. [sglt2, kidney]
23. ★ Worsening heart failure means progressive symptoms, hospitalisation, or
    escalating diuretic need despite stable GDMT; it carries 23% two-year
    mortality and 56% readmission within 30 days, and non-adherent patients
    do not count. [worsening-hf]
24. ★ Referral to an advanced heart failure team is for inotrope need or
    defibrillator shocks, worsening kidney or liver function, persistent
    hypotension, more than two admissions in 6 months, inability to titrate
    GDMT, ejection fraction under 20% with persistent class III or IV
    symptoms, or persistently high natriuretic peptides; palliative care is
    indicated with cardiac cachexia (5% unintentional weight loss in a
    year), frequent admissions, or ineligibility for transplant.
    [advanced-hf, palliative]

Also from this section, kept as context: follow-up is every 3 to 6 months,
every 2 weeks during titration, and within 1 to 2 weeks of a discharge;
repeat echo 3 to 6 months after optimising GDMT, though remodelling can take
a year; drugs that cause or worsen heart failure (NSAIDs, nondihydropyridine
calcium channel blockers, thiazolidinediones, saxagliptin, class IC and III
antiarrhythmics, and others) should be stopped where possible.

## Notes for question writing

- Topic A has the most numbers that drive decisions (washout, titration
  windows, NNTs). Fixed-order numeric questions will be common here.
- Topic C is the natural home for multiple-answer questions: indications for
  each secondary drug, which drugs to cut first for hypotension, referral
  criteria, potassium thresholds.
- Topic B's bedside-sign accuracy numbers make good "which sign is most
  useful" questions on the as-dx-01 model. Do not ask which signs are
  unreliable; nearly all are.
- Topic C facts 18, 19 and 21 are all thresholds; split each into single
  questions per the one-idea rule, and keep the explanations from citing
  each other's numbers.
- If three planets from one article feels like too much cardiology in one
  run, Topic C could be split into "secondary therapies and devices" and
  "adverse effects", giving four, or A and B could merge. I'd keep three.
