# Fact sheet: ABFM 2025 In-Training Examination

Source: the 2025 ITE question book and critique book, 200 items. Supplied as
PDFs on 2026-10-09. The PDFs are copyrighted and stay out of the repo; this
sheet records the lessons in our own words, and every question written from
it cites its item number so the original can be checked during review.

Status: first pass, items 1 to 200. Second pass, which
merges duplicates by area and writes a frame per topic, is deferred until
more items are in.

How to read this: one entry per exam item. The area tag is provisional. The
★ line is the lesson of the correct answer. "Why not" lines are the lessons
behind the explained distractors, which become their own questions when the
critique gives a real reason. "Pivot" is the fact in the vignette that made
the answer right and the runner-up wrong, when there is one.

## Item 1. Quality improvement [practice]

- ★ The first step of a QI project is finding out why performance is low;
  interventions chosen before the barriers are known tend to miss.
- Why not posters: general materials move screening rates less than a direct
  clinician recommendation.
- Why not blanket reminders: patient-specific reminders at the point of care
  beat general reminders to patients or clinicians.
- Pivot: "first step" in the stem, not "most effective intervention".

## Item 2. Prediabetes [endocrine]

- ★ An intensive lifestyle and behaviour programme (the Diabetes Prevention
  Programme model) is the intervention with the best evidence for
  preventing progression to diabetes; one US cohort showed 27% fewer cases
  over 15 years.
- Programme targets: at least 7% weight loss, 150 minutes a week of
  activity including up to 75 of strength training, a 500 to 1000 kcal/day
  deficit.
- Why not low-carbohydrate diet: dietary pattern evidence is weaker than
  the programme evidence.
- Why not 250 kcal/day: half the deficit the programmes use.

## Item 3. Fifth metatarsal shaft fracture [musculoskeletal]

- ★ A fifth metatarsal shaft fracture is treated weight-bearing in a boot or
  hard-soled shoe for 3 to 6 weeks.
- Why not a short leg cast: casts are for proximal fifth metatarsal and
  tarsal fractures.
- Why not orthopaedics: referral is for fractures needing reduction, or
  nonunion at 6 months.
- Why not physiotherapy: not first-line for an acute fracture.
- Context: foot fractures are about a third of adult lower-limb fractures;
  complications include nonunion, malunion, arthritis, infection and
  compartment syndrome.

## Item 4. Septic shock [acute]

- ★ When the mean arterial pressure stays under 65 mm Hg after 30 mL/kg of
  crystalloid, start norepinephrine; it is the first-line vasopressor.
- Bundle: 30 mL/kg crystalloid in the first 3 hours, antibiotics within 1
  hour.
- Why not dopamine: norepinephrine is the more potent vasoconstrictor and
  is preferred.
- Why not vasopressin first: it is added when norepinephrine alone leaves
  the pressure inadequate.
- Why not a central line first: vasopressors are not delayed for one.
- Why not more fluid: further boluses after the initial 30 mL/kg are
  controversial and judged by response, in 500 mL steps.
- Pivot: MAP 58 after the full initial bolus.

## Item 5. Thyroid storm [endocrine, acute]

- ★ The first drug in thyroid storm is propylthiouracil.
- PTU lowers T3 faster than methimazole, but the two are clinically
  equivalent in outcome.
- Why not iodine first: iodine blocks new hormone synthesis but is given at
  least 1 hour after the thionamide, or it feeds the gland.
- Why not prednisone: glucocorticoids block T4 to T3 conversion, and
  hydrocortisone or dexamethasone are the ones used.
- Why not plasmapheresis: reserved for failure of drug treatment.

## Item 6. Diarrhoea-predominant IBS [gastrointestinal]

- ★ Rifaximin has the strongest evidence for the typical symptoms of IBS-D
  (diarrhoea, pain, bloating); a 14-day course, repeatable for recurrence;
  few adverse effects, high cost.
- Why not amitriptyline or dicyclomine: often help, weaker evidence.
- Why not linaclotide or lubiprostone: both are IBS-C drugs; linaclotide
  causes diarrhoea and many stop it.

## Item 7. Painful diabetic neuropathy [endocrine, pain]

- ★ First-line drugs: amitriptyline, duloxetine, gabapentin, pregabalin.
- Why not topical lidocaine or capsaicin: second-line, less evidence.
- Why not hydrocodone: opioids are avoided in neuropathic pain.
- Why not TENS: modest benefit, not first-line.

## Item 8. Urinary incontinence, initial evaluation [genitourinary]

- ★ Every woman presenting with urinary incontinence gets a urinalysis.
- Initial evaluation is history (transient or chronic, which subtype, red
  flags), a screening questionnaire, cough stress test, post-void residual,
  3-day voiding diary.
- Red flags for referral: pelvic organ prolapse, fistula.
- Why not ultrasound, urodynamics or cystoscopy: not part of the initial
  work-up of uncomplicated incontinence.
- Risk factors: obesity, parity, age, hysterectomy.
- Treatment ladder: weight loss, pelvic floor training, fluid and smoking
  advice, drugs; refractory cases go on to devices, bladder botulinum
  toxin, surgery.

## Item 9. Immune thrombocytopenia [haematology]

- ★ ITP is not treated unless there is active bleeding or the platelet
  count is under 30,000/mm³.
- Treatments when needed: corticosteroids, IVIG, rituximab.
- Why not hydroxychloroquine: not a standard ITP treatment.
- Pivot: 47,000 and no bleeding.

## Item 10. Long-term monitoring on antiretroviral therapy [infection]

- ★ Stable patients on ART: urinalysis at least yearly and a GFR at least
  twice yearly, for drug nephrotoxicity.
- Why not Framingham: not validated in HIV; use traditional risk factors
  and judgement.
- Why not annual neurocognitive screening: by clinical indication only.
- Why not annual elastography: fibrosis screening is for hepatitis B or C
  coinfection.
- Why not annual DXA: bone density matters in HIV but is not an annual
  test for everyone.

## Item 11. Chest pain after a negative work-up [cardiovascular]

- ★ Intermediate-risk patient, no known CAD, angina, negative troponins,
  negative stress test within the past year: coronary CT angiography is
  the next test, to find obstructive disease or plaque.
- Why not repeat stress testing: already negative within a year.
- Why not FFR-CT: used after CT angiography shows a stenosis, to guide
  revascularisation.
- Why not cardiac MRI: for structural questions.

## Item 12. Alcohol use disorder, maintaining abstinence [psychiatry]

- ★ Acamprosate is for patients who have already stopped drinking and want
  to stay stopped (SOR A).
- Gabapentin prevents withdrawal and is safe in people still drinking.
- Topiramate reduces heavy drinking (SOR B).
- Why not disulfiram: insufficient evidence.
- Fewer than 2% of people with AUD are prescribed any medication.

## Item 13. Poison ivy dermatitis [dermatology]

- ★ Facial, genital or significant bullous involvement needs systemic
  steroids: prednisone 1 mg/kg (max 60 mg) tapered over 15 days.
- Why not a 5-day methylprednisolone pack: too short; rebound follows.
- Why not topical clobetasol: potent topicals do nothing for bullae and are
  contraindicated on face and genitals.
- Why not subcutaneous triamcinolone: if used at all it is intramuscular;
  subcutaneous causes lipoatrophy, and onset is slower and variable.
- Why not draining bullae: no indication.
- Context: type IV hypersensitivity to urushiol, onset 12 to 72 hours,
  needs prior sensitisation. Blister fluid does not spread it; the
  apparent spread is different resin doses giving different onset times.

## Item 14. Incidental pulmonary nodule [respiratory]

- ★ A solid nodule of 6 to 8 mm is followed with CT at 6 to 12 months.
- Intervals flex (3 to 6, 6 to 12) for nodule features and preference.
- A solid nodule unchanged for 2 years is benign.
- Why not referral: pulmonology is for solid nodules 8 to 30 mm or any
  subsolid nodule.
- Why not biopsy: not for a small solid nodule in a non-smoker.

## Item 15. Suspected upper GI bleed, stable [gastrointestinal, acute]

- ★ A stable patient with a probable upper GI bleed gets a proton pump
  inhibitor at presentation, regardless of endoscopy timing.
- Clue set: epigastric pain, nausea, NSAIDs with alcohol, raised BUN,
  anaemia.
- Why not urgent endoscopy: urgent only with haemodynamic instability.
- Why not nasogastric lavage: no trial or guideline support.
- Why not transfusion: threshold is under 7 g/dL, or under 8 with coronary
  disease, recent cardiac surgery or haematological malignancy.
- Why not embolisation: only after endoscopy fails.

## Item 16. Fluoride for infants [paediatrics]

- ★ From the first tooth, brush once or twice daily with a rice-sized
  smear of fluoride toothpaste; pea-sized from age 3.
- Varnish from the first tooth: every 3 months at high caries risk, every
  6 months otherwise.
- Why not mouthwash: not before age 6, and then only if the child can
  spit.
- Supplements are for children whose water is not fluoridated.
- Swallowed toothpaste causes fluorosis; children usually stop swallowing
  by 6.

## Item 17. Parkinson disease, first drug [neurology]

- ★ First-line: carbidopa/levodopa, MAO-B inhibitors, non-ergot dopamine
  agonists.
- Why not propranolol: that treats essential tremor.
- Why not amitriptyline or fluoxetine: wrong diagnosis, and both carry
  Beers Criteria cautions in older adults.
- Why not gabapentin: partial seizures and post-herpetic neuralgia.

## Item 18. Pituitary adenoma, which hormone [endocrine]

- ★ Growing hands and feet, coarsening face, carpal tunnel, menstrual
  change, with an adenoma: a somatotroph adenoma, confirmed by a raised
  IGF-1.
- Corticotroph adenoma (Cushing disease): mood change, weight gain, easy
  bruising, proximal weakness; abnormal dexamethasone suppression test.
- Lactotroph adenoma: galactorrhoea, low libido, amenorrhoea; raised
  prolactin.
- Thyrotroph adenoma: hyperthyroid symptoms with a raised TSH and raised
  T4.

## Item 19. Vasomotor symptoms of menopause [womens-health]

- ★ Oral or transdermal oestrogen is the most effective treatment for hot
  flashes (SOR A); after hysterectomy no progestogen is needed.
- SSRIs and SNRIs are effective alternatives; paroxetine is FDA-approved
  for the indication.
- Why not vaginal oestrogen: for vaginal symptoms, not vasomotor.
- Why not testosterone: ineffective for vasomotor symptoms.
- Why not soy: inconsistent evidence (SOR B).

## Item 20. Refusal of blood with capacity [ethics]

- ★ A patient with capacity, or an advance directive, who refuses blood is
  not transfused, even when death is the likely result and the physician
  disagrees.
- Blood refusal directives are legally binding; physicians have been held
  liable for overriding them.
- Why not family consent: the family cannot override the patient's own
  refusal.
- Why not packed cells instead of whole blood: a product substitution does
  not get round the refusal.
- Transfusion may be permissible only for an incapacitated patient with no
  directive and no surrogate.

## Item 21. Epistaxis on warfarin [ent, acute]

- ★ Haemodynamically stable epistaxis: pinch the lower third of the nose
  for 15 to 20 minutes first.
- Next if that fails and an anterior source is seen: topical
  vasoconstrictor or cautery; then anterior packing.
- Why not reverse anticoagulation: considered for ongoing bleeding with
  haemodynamic instability.
- Pivot: stable, no orthostasis.

## Item 22. Stable angina, adding a drug [cardiovascular]

- ★ Angina persisting on a maximally tolerated first-line drug: add one
  from another first-line class (beta blocker, calcium channel blocker,
  long-acting nitrate). Here, with a heart rate of 52, a nitrate.
- Why not more beta blocker: relative bradycardia.
- Why not ivabradine: contraindicated in chronic coronary disease with a
  normal ejection fraction, and potentially harmful.
- Why not clopidogrel or more empagliflozin: neither relieves angina.
- Ranolazine is for patients already on two other classes; costly.

## Item 23. Acute insomnia and CBT-I [psychiatry]

- Acute insomnia: trouble initiating or maintaining sleep at least 3 nights
  a week for 1 week to 3 months, usually with a precipitant.
- First step: identify and treat the precipitant.
- ★ CBT-I has three parts. Sleep hygiene education (fixed wake time, no
  daytime naps, among eight items); stimulus control (bed only when sleepy,
  bedroom for sleep and sex); sleep restriction (cut time in bed, then add
  15 minutes a week once sleep efficiency reaches 90%).
- Why not "decrease time in bed" as hygiene: that is sleep restriction.

## Item 24. Subclinical hypothyroidism [endocrine]

- ★ Non-pregnant subclinical hypothyroidism (TSH raised but under 10, free
  T4 normal) is treated only if thyroid peroxidase antibodies are raised or
  the TSH is over 10 (SOR A).
- Treatment-resistant depression is also an accepted indication;
  treatment-resistant constipation is not.
- Why not a repeat TSH of 6 or 8: still under 10, still not an indication.
- Why not T3: not routinely measured.

## Item 25. Acute heavy uterine bleeding, stable [womens-health, acute]

- ★ Severe acute uterine bleeding in a haemodynamically stable patient:
  intravenous tranexamic acid.
- Why not combined oral contraceptive or depot medroxyprogesterone: recent
  breast cancer makes hormonal options ones to avoid where possible.
- Why not intravenous oestrogen: reserved for the unstable patient, because
  of clot risk.
- Why not a levonorgestrel IUD: good long-term treatment, impractical in
  the emergency department, and 3 to 6 months before bleeding improves.

## Item 26. Rosacea, first drug [dermatology]

- ★ When skincare and sun protection fail, first-line is topical
  metronidazole; topical azelaic acid is the other first-line choice.
- Why not oral doxycycline: the next step after topicals fail; its
  photosensitivity is a particular nuisance because rosacea flares in the
  warm months.
- Why not brimonidine: second-line, costly, and irritates the skin.
- Why not laser or light therapy, or topical cyclosporine: second-line, for
  failure of the topicals.

## Item 27. Cervical screening after 65 [preventive]

- ★ Screening stops after 65 only with adequate prior screening: three
  consecutive negative cytology results, or two consecutive negative
  co-tests, within the past 10 years, the most recent within 5 years.
- A 67-year-old whose last test was 15 years ago is not adequately
  screened and needs either two negative co-tests 5 years apart or three
  negative cytology results 3 years apart before stopping.
- Pivot: age alone does not end screening; the screening history does.

## Item 28. Positive PHQ-9 item 9 [psychiatry]

- ★ "Thoughts of being better off dead" nearly every day, PHQ-9 of 19: the
  next step is a suicide risk assessment (plan, means, mental illness,
  substance use, stressors) before any treatment is started.
- Up to half of patients labelled low risk go on to complete suicide.
- SSRIs with CBT are first-line for depression with suicidality, once risk
  is assessed.
- Lithium reduces suicide attempts and all-cause mortality in major
  depression and bipolar disorder, but not before a diagnosis is made.
- Ketamine improves depression and suicidality within 24 hours, but the
  evidence is low quality and the effect fades by 2 weeks; reserved for
  resistant depression.
- Why not the emergency department: transfer is for a plan or other
  high-risk features, often as a bridge to an inpatient bed.

## Item 29. Surveillance after small adenomas [gastroenterology]

- ★ One or two tubular adenomas under 10 mm: repeat colonoscopy in 7 to 10
  years (moderate-quality evidence).
- Three or four small adenomas: 3 to 5 years. Five to ten: 3 years. More
  than ten: yearly.
- Why not FIT or stool DNA: in the United States only colonoscopy is used
  for surveillance after polyps.
- Screening continues to at least 75 in a healthy patient (SOR A).

## Item 30. Induction at 39 weeks, the ARRIVE trial [obstetrics]

- ★ Induction at 39 weeks in low-risk nulliparas lowered the caesarean rate
  (18.6% versus 22.2%).
- Also lower: gestational hypertension and preeclampsia (9.1% versus
  14.1%) and neonatal respiratory support in the first 72 hours.
- No difference in the composite neonatal outcome, NICU admission, or
  postpartum haemorrhage.
- Why not low birth weight: median birth weight was lower with induction;
  the rate of low birth weight was not reduced.
- Why not shorter stay: induction lengthened time on labour and delivery
  (median 20 versus 14 hours).

## Item 31. Hip osteoarthritis, pain relief [musculoskeletal]

- ★ Intra-articular corticosteroid injection relieves hip osteoarthritis
  pain for at least 4 months and can improve activity and quality of life.
- Why not hyaluronic acid: not substantially beneficial in the hip.
- Why not a lidocaine patch: no evidence in hip osteoarthritis.
- Why not hydrocodone: opioids are advised against in chronic
  osteoarthritis, ineffective long term, high misuse risk.
- Why not tramadol: no benefit for pain or function in the hip, with the
  same risks as other opioids.
- Context: groin pain with FADIR testing is the hip joint.

## Item 32. Transient global amnesia [neurology]

- ★ Abrupt anterograde amnesia with repetitive questioning, intact identity,
  and a normal examination and CT, after intercourse or another intense
  stress: transient global amnesia. Treatment is reassurance.
- Resolves within about 6 hours.
- Why not anticoagulants, antiplatelets, benzodiazepines or thrombolysis:
  the mechanism is unrelated to seizure, TIA or stroke.

## Item 33. Lyme disease without a rash, doxycycline intolerant [infection]

- ★ Amoxicillin 500 mg three times daily for 14 to 21 days is the
  alternative for patients who cannot take doxycycline or are pregnant;
  cefuroxime is the other option.
- Without erythema migrans, diagnosis is two-tier serology (enzyme
  immunoassay, then Western blot); with the rash it is clinical (SOR C).
- Why not ceftriaxone: intravenous ceftriaxone is for late neurological
  Lyme disease.
- Why not chloramphenicol: that is the doxycycline alternative for Rocky
  Mountain spotted fever.
- A course the patient can finish matters; partial treatment risks later
  complications.

## Item 34. Recombinant zoster vaccine after shingles [preventive]

- ★ A recent episode of shingles does not delay the recombinant vaccine
  once the rash has resolved; postherpetic neuralgia need not have
  resolved. Prior live zoster vaccine is also no bar.
- Evidence after a prior episode is indirect, but the recurrence risk is
  real and the vaccine is safe and immunogenic.

## Item 35. Orbital cellulitis [ophthalmology]

- ★ Lid swelling with pain on eye movement and reduced visual acuity is
  orbital cellulitis until proven otherwise: straight to the emergency
  department for admission, intravenous antibiotics and ophthalmology.
- Imaging of choice is CT with intravenous contrast; MRI is the
  alternative. Non-contrast CT is less accurate.
- Why not same-day ophthalmology clinic, oral antibiotics, or compresses:
  each delays definitive treatment.
- Pivot: pain with extraocular movement and reduced acuity separate orbital
  from preseptal cellulitis; lid erythema and oedema do not.

## Item 36. Acute DVT with a GFR of 22 [haematology]

- ★ Outpatient low molecular weight heparin, renally dosed: enoxaparin
  1 mg/kg once daily rather than twice daily when the GFR is under 30.
- Why not rivaroxaban: direct oral anticoagulants are not used below a GFR
  of 30; and its loading phase is 15 mg twice daily for 21 days, not 14.
- Why not dabigatran: higher bleeding risk.
- Why not warfarin alone: not for the acute phase.
- Why not admission for heparin: uncomplicated DVT is treated at home.

## Item 37. Chronic bacterial prostatitis [genitourinary]

- ★ Months of pelvic pain, a boggy tender prostate, pyuria, negative STI
  testing: chronic bacterial prostatitis, treated with a fluoroquinolone.
- Fluoroquinolones cover the usual uropathogens, penetrate prostatic
  tissue, and are well absorbed orally.
- Why not amoxicillin or ceftriaxone: poor choices for prostatitis.
- Why not CT or urology: the diagnosis is clinical and the management is
  within family medicine.

## Item 38. Small primary pneumothorax [respiratory]

- ★ Stable patient, pneumothorax under 15% of the hemithorax: oxygen by
  nasal cannula, observe at least 6 hours, repeat the chest film.
- Stable at 6 hours: home, with review at 24 hours.
- Why not CT: only when the film is equivocal.
- Why not needle decompression or a chest tube: for the unstable patient,
  a large pneumothorax, or severe symptoms; decompression is followed by a
  tube.

## Item 39. Preparticipation evaluation [sports medicine]

- ★ An acutely enlarged spleen or liver is a contraindication to contact
  sport because of rupture risk, and needs a cause found first.
- Why not obesity: raises heat-illness risk, does not restrict play.
- Why not insulin-dependent diabetes: exercise is encouraged, with insulin
  and meal adjustment.
- Why not asymptomatic mild mitral valve prolapse: no restriction.
- Why not tibial tubercle apophysitis: activity is encouraged with
  conservative management.

## Item 40. Meniere disease [ENT]

- ★ Spontaneous recurring vertigo lasting hours, with unilateral fullness,
  tinnitus and hearing loss: Meniere disease. Audiometry is the test to
  order, though there is no diagnostic test as such.
- Acute attacks: a vestibular suppressant such as promethazine, diazepam or
  prochlorperazine.
- Why not the Epley manoeuvre: that is for benign paroxysmal positional
  vertigo.
- Why not a diuretic, corticosteroid or antiviral: no good-quality
  evidence that any treats or prevents it.
- Why not alcohol reduction: salt, alcohol and caffeine restriction are
  traditional, without sufficient evidence.

## Item 41. Number needed to treat [evidence]

- ★ NNT is 1 divided by the absolute risk reduction. Control event rate
  0.085, treatment 0.045: ARR 0.04, NNT 25.

## Item 42. Neurogenic orthostatic hypotension [cardiovascular]

- ★ Midodrine is the safest and best-tolerated single agent (SOR B).
- Why not atomoxetine: an add-on to midodrine, not effective alone.
- Why not fludrocortisone: off-label, with risks of left ventricular
  hypertrophy, renal failure and hypokalaemia.
- Why not methylphenidate: no demonstrated role.
- Why not sodium tablets: usually inadequate, and risk hypernatraemia and
  renal injury.

## Item 43. DKA, adding glucose [endocrine]

- ★ Glucose is added to the infusion once plasma glucose reaches 201 to
  250 mg/dL, to hold it at 150 to 200 while insulin continues.
- The reason: acidosis and ketosis clear more slowly than hyperglycaemia,
  so insulin must go on after the glucose is controlled.
- (The threshold itself is already in Foundations, fd-dka-06.)

## Item 44. PTSD nightmares [psychiatry]

- ★ Midsleep awakening from hyperarousal and nightmares in PTSD: prazosin.
- Why not amitriptyline, mirtazapine or risperidone: side-effect burden
  makes them poor first choices for the sleep symptom.
- Why not temazepam: benzodiazepines are avoided for misuse and long-term
  harm.
- Note: the critique calls prazosin an alpha-2 agonist; it is an alpha-1
  antagonist. Not used in any question.

## Item 45. Thiamine deficiency after bariatric surgery [nutrition]

- ★ Symmetric sensory and motor neuropathy (burning feet, absent reflexes,
  weakness) with nausea and confusion after bariatric surgery: beriberi.
  Blood or urine thiamine confirms it.
- Risk factors: alcohol use, bariatric surgery, older age, diabetes, HIV.
- Severe deficiency causes heart failure.
- Why not diabetic neuropathy: usually sensory only; motor involvement is
  for severe cases.
- Why not Lyme: peripheral neuropathy is rare and is a mononeuropathy;
  Lyme neurology is meningitis, facial palsy, radiculoneuritis.
- Why not ME/CFS: follows an infection, no neuropathy.
- Why not secondary syphilis: rash and constitutional symptoms; ocular and
  otic involvement, not neuropathy.

## Item 46. Knee injury in a 76-year-old [musculoskeletal]

- ★ Ottawa knee rule: age 55 or older alone indicates a radiograph after a
  knee injury, whatever the examination shows.
- The other criteria: isolated patellar tenderness, fibular head
  tenderness, inability to flex to 90 degrees, inability to bear weight
  for four steps.
- Why not MRI: ligament and meniscal tests were negative.
- Why not ultrasound: radiography is preferred for fracture in adults.
- Why not arthrocentesis: for a suspected joint infection.

## Item 47. Rocky Mountain spotted fever in pregnancy [infection]

- ★ Doxycycline for everyone with presumed RMSF, pregnant or not; short
  courses of 5 to 10 days are safe in pregnancy and in children.
- Testing is not done first; RMSF can be fatal and testing delays
  treatment.
- Why not chloramphenicol: an option, but with a higher death rate.
- Why not amoxicillin or erythromycin: no role.
- True doxycycline allergy: inpatient rapid desensitisation.

## Item 48. Cholestasis with antimitochondrial antibodies [hepatology]

- ★ Raised alkaline phosphatase, positive antimitochondrial antibodies,
  pruritus and fatigue: primary biliary cholangitis. Diagnosis needs two
  of cholestatic enzymes, antimitochondrial antibodies, and nonsuppurative
  destructive cholangitis on biopsy. AMA is positive in over 95%.
- Why not autoimmune hepatitis: raised aminotransferases with antinuclear
  or anti-smooth muscle antibodies, not cholestasis.
- Why not MASLD: raised aminotransferases with metabolic syndrome.
- Why not primary sclerosing cholangitis: cholestatic too, but linked to
  inflammatory bowel disease, diagnosed by strictures on MR
  cholangiography or ERCP, and rarely AMA positive.
- Why not drug-induced injury: chronicity and the antibody make it
  unlikely.

## Item 49. Mixed features, bipolar disorder [psychiatry]

- ★ Irritability, restlessness, spending, infidelity, then depression with
  past similar episodes: bipolar disorder. Treat with lithium, an
  anticonvulsant, or an atypical antipsychotic such as quetiapine.
- Why not an SSRI or bupropion alone: antidepressant monotherapy is
  contraindicated with mixed features, mania, or bipolar I.
- Why not ECT: effective for psychotic depression, mania, refractory
  illness, catatonia, acute psychosis and suicidal behaviour, but drugs
  come first.
- Augmenting therapies (omega-3, light, ketamine, probiotics) lack
  evidence; CBT, exercise and nutrition help.

## Item 50. Aspirin to prevent preeclampsia [obstetrics]

- ★ One high-risk factor or two moderate-risk factors: aspirin 81 mg daily
  from 12 weeks.
- High-risk: prior preeclampsia, multifetal gestation, chronic
  hypertension, type 1 or 2 diabetes, kidney disease, autoimmune disease.
- Moderate: first pregnancy, age 35 or older, BMI over 30, family history
  of preeclampsia, sociodemographic and personal history factors.
- Nulliparous with a BMI of 33 is two moderate factors.
- Also reduces growth restriction and preterm birth, with a good safety
  profile.

## Item 51. Anticoagulation after cardioembolic stroke [cardiovascular]

- ★ Small cardioembolic stroke or TIA with nonvalvular atrial fibrillation:
  anticoagulation alone, no antiplatelet, started 2 to 14 days after the
  event; a direct oral anticoagulant over warfarin.
- Nonvalvular means no moderate or severe mitral stenosis and no
  mechanical valve; mitral regurgitation does not count.
- The 2024 OPTIMAS trial showed starting within 4 days is safe.
- Larger strokes at risk of haemorrhagic conversion wait up to 14 days.
- (The long-term choice of anticoagulant is already in Foundations,
  fd-str-04; questions here are on timing and the definition.)

## Item 52. Papillary muscle rupture after STEMI [cardiovascular]

- ★ Day 3 to 5 after a transmural circumflex or right coronary infarct:
  sudden dyspnoea, hypotension, a new systolic murmur, crackles throughout
  and no raised neck veins is papillary muscle rupture with acute mitral
  regurgitation and flash pulmonary oedema.
- Why not tamponade: free wall rupture is rare; chest pain, muffled heart
  sounds and distended neck veins are its clues.
- Why not pericarditis: chest pain is its core feature.
- Why not pulmonary embolism: an anticoagulated inpatient without leg
  oedema makes it unlikely.
- Why not an SVT: a rate of 110 does not produce this degree of collapse.
- Mortality of mechanical complications stays high even as their incidence
  has fallen.

## Item 53. Mild asthma under GINA [respiratory]

- ★ Symptoms fewer than 3 to 5 days a week with normal or mildly reduced
  lung function is mild asthma; from age 12, as-needed low-dose
  ICS-formoterol is the treatment. (Already in Foundations, fd-ast-01.)
- As-needed ICS-formoterol cuts severe exacerbations by 60% to 64%
  compared with as-needed albuterol, and beats every regimen that uses a
  short-acting reliever.
- Why not formoterol alone: formoterol is never used without an inhaled
  corticosteroid.

## Item 54. Cutaneous larva migrans [infection, dermatology]

- ★ An intensely itchy, red, serpiginous rash on the feet after travel to
  Asia or Africa: cutaneous larva migrans. Diagnosed clinically; no test.
- Self-limited; albendazole if treated. Incubation 1 to 5 days, up to a
  month.
- Why not leishmaniasis: painless papules becoming non-healing ulcers with
  raised borders.
- Why not herpes B: vesicles at the inoculation site with flu-like illness.
- Why not scabies: itchy red bumps along skin folds, often in a line.
- Note: the critique names macaques as the reservoir; that is herpes B.
  Larva migrans is dog and cat hookworm. Not used in a question.

## Item 55. Cryptosporidium and swimming [infection, paediatrics]

- ★ Oocyst shedding continues after recovery: no swimming until 2 weeks
  after symptoms resolve.
- Oocysts survive up to 10 days in treated water. Diagnosis is on stool.
- Treatment is fluids and electrolytes; nitazoxanide for severe or
  prolonged disease.

## Item 56. HELLP syndrome [obstetrics]

- ★ Headache, malaise, right upper quadrant pain, hypertension,
  proteinuria, platelets 43,000, raised transaminases at 36 weeks: HELLP.
  Over 34 weeks the answer is urgent delivery.
- Maternal morbidity and mortality 10% to 15%: cerebral haemorrhage,
  cardiopulmonary arrest, DIC, ARDS, renal failure, sepsis, hepatic
  haemorrhage.
- Why not corticosteroids: raise the platelet count, do nothing for the
  liver.
- Why not N-acetylcysteine: the liver failure is from the pregnancy, not
  the acetaminophen.
- Why not gastroenterology: delay.

## Item 57. Confirming chronic kidney disease [renal]

- ★ Chronicity needs 3 months: a second eGFR under 60 three months on, or
  albuminuria persisting 3 months. (The 3-month rule is in Foundations,
  fd-ckd-01.)
- Other evidence of chronicity: prior documentation, imaging showing small
  kidneys or multiple cysts.
- Why not 1 or 2 months: too short to separate it from acute kidney
  injury.
- Why not an albumin-to-creatinine ratio today: one value does not
  establish persistence.
- Note: the critique says "cortical thickening"; chronic kidneys show
  cortical thinning. Not used in a question.

## Item 58. Kawasaki disease [paediatrics]

- ★ Fever for 5 days or more with 4 of: oral mucous membrane changes,
  bilateral conjunctivitis, diffuse rash, hand and foot oedema, a
  unilateral cervical node of 1.5 cm or more. The serious complication is
  cardiac: coronary dilation and aneurysm, aortic root dilation, shock.
  Echocardiography as soon as possible.
- Untreated, 15% to 25% develop cardiovascular abnormalities.
- Lesser complications: anaemia, raised liver enzymes, pyuria, pulmonary
  oedema.

## Item 59. BRCA testing [preventive]

- ★ No personal or family history of breast, ovarian, tubal or peritoneal
  cancer: reassurance. The task force recommends against routine risk
  assessment, counselling or testing (D).
- Risk assessment tools are for women with such a history or an ancestry
  linked to the mutations. Testing follows a positive tool and genetic
  counselling, in that order.
- Why not the friend's BRCA2, the father's colorectal cancer, the aunt's
  thyroid cancer or the grandmother's endometrial cancer: none qualifies.

## Item 60. High-dose folic acid [obstetrics]

- ★ High-risk pregnancy (family history of neural tube defect): 4 mg daily
  until 12 weeks after conception, then 0.4 mg daily.
- The dose drops because organogenesis is largely complete and there is a
  theoretical concern about fetal toxicity.
- Supplementation starts 1 to 3 months before conception, on top of
  dietary folate.

## Item 61. Starting urate-lowering therapy [rheumatology]

- ★ Recurrent gout with CKD stage 3: allopurinol is first-line even with
  kidney disease, started with colchicine to prevent a flare (strong ACR
  recommendation). Titrate to a urate under 6 mg/dL.
- Colchicine, NSAIDs or corticosteroids alone are for the acute flare.
- Why not febuxostat: a xanthine oxidase inhibitor for chronic gout, not
  first-line.
- Why not pegloticase: strongly recommended against as first-line for
  cost, uncertain safety and better alternatives.

## Item 62. Aortic stenosis, referral [cardiovascular]

- ★ New angina in aortic stenosis is an indication for valve replacement;
  symptomatic patients have under 50% two-year survival without it, and
  replacement restores survival to that of age-matched adults.
- Patients with angina are also evaluated for coronary disease.
- Why not calcification, mitral insufficiency, LVH on a film, or a louder
  murmur: none is an indication by itself.
- (Symptom-driven referral is covered by the Articles set; the question
  here is the coronary evaluation.)

## Item 63. Mild allergic rhinitis [ENT]

- ★ Intranasal corticosteroids are first-line even for mild disease.
- Intranasal or oral antihistamines are less effective; cromolyn is less
  effective and needs several doses a day.
- Saline irrigation has limited evidence and augments rather than replaces
  drug treatment.
- No antibiotics without infection; no oral steroids.

## Item 64. Diagnosing PCOS [endocrine, womens-health]

- ★ Rotterdam: two of oligo-anovulation, hyperandrogenism (clinical or
  biochemical, such as raised free testosterone) and polycystic ovaries on
  ultrasound.
- Mimics to exclude: androgen-secreting tumour, exogenous androgens,
  Cushing syndrome, nonclassical congenital adrenal hyperplasia,
  hypothalamic amenorrhoea, ovarian insufficiency, acromegaly, thyroid
  and prolactin disorders.
- Why not 17-hydroxyprogesterone: raised in congenital adrenal
  hyperplasia.
- Why not cortisol: raised in Cushing syndrome or an adrenal tumour.
- Why not insulin or glucose: part of the pathophysiology, not the
  criteria.

## Item 65. Vestibular neuritis [ENT, neurology]

- ★ Spontaneous continuous rotatory vertigo for a day or more, listing to
  one side, horizontal nystagmus, a saccade on head impulse, no hearing
  loss: vestibular neuritis. Reassurance; antiemetics for up to 3 days;
  vestibular rehabilitation if it drags on.
- Usually viral, ages 30 to 50, resolves in days.
- Why not Epley: that is for BPPV, which is brief and positional (SOR A
  against it here).
- Why not steroids or antivirals: not shown effective.
- Why not MRI: for central signs or hearing loss (SOR C).

## Item 66. Melasma after pregnancy [dermatology]

- ★ The most effective topical treatment is the triple combination of
  fluocinolone, hydroquinone and tretinoin, as one formulation or as
  separate components.
- Melasma affects up to 75% of pregnancies and resolves postpartum in 90%;
  it can recur with later pregnancies or oral contraception.
- Why not azelaic acid or benzoyl peroxide: acne drugs, ineffective here.
- Why not imiquimod: for condyloma; no role.

## Item 67. Introducing allergenic foods [paediatrics]

- ★ Peanut and egg are introduced at 4 to 6 months, which gives the lowest
  risk of later allergy.
- Peanut butter thinned with water, breast milk, cow's milk or formula;
  never whole peanuts (choking). Eggs well cooked.

## Item 68. After sexual assault [womens-health]

- ★ Offer forensic examination, emergency contraception, and empiric
  treatment for gonorrhoea, chlamydia and trichomonas: ceftriaxone,
  azithromycin and metronidazole or tinidazole.
- HIV post-exposure prophylaxis is triple therapy for 28 days, not the
  two-drug PrEP regimen.
- Emergency contraception: ulipristal, levonorgestrel or the copper IUD;
  long-term contraception can follow.
- Why not hepatitis C prophylaxis: not recommended.

## Item 69. Premenstrual syndrome, an SSRI already on board [womens-health]

- ★ Calcium carbonate 1000 to 1200 mg daily reduces the mood symptoms of
  PMS (SOR B).
- First-line for PMS and PMDD: any SSRI, and continuous combined oral
  contraceptives (SOR A).
- Why not bupropion: not shown effective.
- Why not dutasteride: high dose helps PMDD but harms mood and is
  teratogenic; low dose does nothing.
- Why not vitamin D: levels are not low in PMS and supplementing does not
  clearly help.
- Why not acupuncture: insufficient evidence.

## Item 70. Oral bisphosphonate not tolerated [musculoskeletal]

- ★ Alendronate stopped for gastrointestinal symptoms in a woman with a
  T-score of minus 2.8: move to an intravenous bisphosphonate.
- Correct administration first: empty stomach, 8 oz of water, upright and
  nothing else for 30 minutes. (Already in Foundations, fd-ost-05.)
- Why not raloxifene: not the preferred initial treatment.

## Item 71. Bacterial meningitis, adjunct [infection]

- ★ Intravenous dexamethasone, given shortly before or with the first
  antibiotic dose, improves mortality and neurological sequelae.
- Empiric adult therapy: vancomycin and ceftriaxone.
- Why not acyclovir: for suspected herpes simplex encephalitis.
- Why not furosemide: a septic patient needs fluid.
- Why not glycerol: more deaths in the available adult data.
- Why not immunoglobulin: not a treatment.

## Item 72. Eosinophilic oesophagitis, treatment [gastroenterology]

- ★ Confirmed eosinophilic oesophagitis with partial response to a proton
  pump inhibitor: oral budesonide suspension is first-line; a swallowed
  inhaled steroid is the alternative.
- Diagnosis is histological: more than 15 eosinophils per high-power
  field with other causes excluded. Suspect it in atopic patients with
  heartburn that fails a PPI; men in their 20s and 30s; dysphagia for
  years; food impaction in over half.
- PPIs alone give complete relief in about a third.
- Why not clotrimazole: Candida oesophagitis is treated with fluconazole.
- Why not famotidine: for erosive oesophagitis.
- Why not metoclopramide: abandoned for side effects.
- Why not montelukast: less effective here.

## Item 73. Vaccines on immunosuppression for IBD [preventive]

- ★ An adult with inflammatory bowel disease on methotrexate and
  azathioprine needs a pneumococcal conjugate vaccine.
- Why not MMR: live, contraindicated on immunosuppressive treatment.
- Why not Tdap: given 6 years ago; not due.
- Note: the critique says zoster vaccine waits until 50 and meningococcal
  is for adolescents with IBD. ACIP recommends recombinant zoster from 19
  for immunocompromised adults (as Foundations fd-vac-03 says), so the
  zoster claim conflicts and is not used in a question.

## Item 74. Adrenal crisis [endocrine, acute]

- ★ Known adrenal insufficiency, an infection, vomiting, hypotension,
  tachycardia, sodium 133, potassium 5.8, glucose 60: adrenal crisis.
  Intravenous hydrocortisone 100 mg at once.
- Triggers: acute illness, dehydration, severe physical stress,
  non-adherence.
- Why not dexamethasone: usable but not preferred, and 1 mg is a
  suppression-test dose, far below treatment.
- Why not glucagon: for insulin hypoglycaemia; does not fix the
  circulation.
- Why not ondansetron or an antibiotic: symptom or cause, not the crisis.

## Item 75. Syncope with a normal examination [cardiovascular]

- ★ History, examination and an ECG are the evaluation (SOR C). (Already in
  Foundations, fd-syn-01.)
- EEG is not routine. Carotid ultrasound is not for a normal examination
  (SOR B). Head CT and MRI are not for a normal neurological examination
  (SOR B).
- Syncope with exertion gets an exercise stress test (SOR C); fainting
  while standing in a queue is not exertion.

## Item 76. Lower urinary tract symptoms in a man [genitourinary]

- ★ Storage and voiding symptoms together, urinalysis negative: an
  alpha-blocker (tamsulosin, alfuzosin, terazosin) is first, whichever
  symptom group dominates (level A).
- A 5-alpha-reductase inhibitor is added when the prostate is 30 mL or
  more on imaging, enlarged on examination, or the PSA is over 1.5 ng/mL;
  it takes up to a year to work fully.
- Anticholinergics or a beta-3 agonist, alone or with the alpha-blocker,
  for persistent storage symptoms; check a post-void residual first.
- Daily tadalafil is an option with erectile dysfunction, not first-line.

## Item 77. Is the newborn feeding enough? [paediatrics]

- ★ After day 4, adequate feeding means 6 or more wet nappies and 3 or
  more stools a day; four wet nappies on day 5 is inadequate.
- Weight loss is normal for 3 days; more than 7% from birth weight, or
  loss continuing after day 3, is a concern. Five percent is fine.
- Nipple pain in the first 30 to 60 seconds of a feed is normal; pain
  beyond that needs evaluation.
- Breasts that look small or soft are not a sign of failure, but no breast
  change or leaking at all by day 5 may be.
- Supplementation, if needed, follows the Academy of Breastfeeding
  Medicine protocol.

## Item 78. Influenza B, low risk, late presentation [infection]

- ★ A healthy adult presenting more than 48 hours after onset gets
  supportive care, not an antiviral.
- Neuraminidase inhibitors in outpatients do not reduce hospitalisation or
  death; oseltamivir is first-line for high-risk patients.
- Why not rimantadine: inactive against influenza B, and not first-line
  for A either.
- Why not methylprednisolone: not recommended.

## Item 79. Obsessive-compulsive disorder, first drug [psychiatry]

- ★ SSRIs are the most effective drug class; fluoxetine, fluvoxamine,
  sertraline and paroxetine carry the FDA indication. Higher than usual
  doses are often needed.
- Why not clomipramine: approved for OCD but more toxic, so not
  first-line.
- Why not bupropion, buspirone or quetiapine: not first-line.

## Item 80. Preventing the next fall [geriatrics]

- ★ In older adults at higher fall risk, exercise (gait, balance,
  functional training; individual or group) has the best evidence. (Also
  in Foundations, fd-prev-10.)
- Home hazard reduction using a validated tool, usually by home visit,
  has evidence (2023 Cochrane). Multifactorial assessment and tailored
  intervention gives a small net benefit on average.
- Why not education, a cane, vision correction or footwear assessment:
  not found beneficial.
- A past fall is a key risk factor. In 2018, 27.5% of adults 65 and over
  fell in the year; falls are the leading cause of injury death in older
  adults; over 300,000 hip fracture admissions a year.

## Item 81. Helicobacter pylori, first-line regimen [gastroenterology]

- ★ Optimised bismuth quadruple therapy (bismuth, metronidazole, a proton
  pump inhibitor, tetracycline), about 91% eradication.
- Vonoprazan with amoxicillin is the fallback for those who cannot take
  quadruple therapy; 70% to 80% eradication.
- Clarithromycin or levofloxacin regimens only with documented
  susceptibility.

## Item 82. First aid for a child's scald [emergency]

- ★ Cool running tap water for at least 20 minutes, ideally within 30
  minutes of injury, with benefit up to 3 hours: reduces burn depth,
  speeds healing, reduces grafting.
- Remove clothing to limit the scald.
- Why not ice: vasoconstriction adds tissue damage.
- Why not wet gauze: not an effective cooling strategy.
- Why not aloe vera or an NSAID gel: for pain after cooling, not first.
- Depth: superficial is red, blanching, intact, painful. Superficial
  partial thickness blisters and weeps; deep partial thickness is dry and
  does not blanch. Full thickness is white, dry, waxy, less painful. Deep
  partial and full thickness go to a burn centre.

## Item 83. First DVT, outpatient [haematology]

- ★ A direct oral anticoagulant alone; no heparin lead-in, because DOACs
  reach therapeutic levels quickly. Less major bleeding and less
  monitoring than warfarin. (The preference itself is in Foundations,
  fd-vte-03.)
- If a DOAC is unaffordable: low molecular weight heparin started with
  warfarin as an outpatient, continued until the INR is at goal.
- Inpatient unfractionated heparin with warfarin is the old way.

## Item 84. Breast cancer risk factors [preventive]

- ★ Chest radiotherapy in childhood, especially whole-lung, is the
  highest-risk item among the usual list.
- Other risks: menopause after 55, first pregnancy after 35, a
  first-degree relative with breast cancer, dense breasts (more glandular
  and stromal tissue relative to fat).
- Why not early first pregnancy, early menopause, fatty breasts, or a
  grandmother: the opposite of risk, or too distant.

## Item 85. Post-stroke depression [psychiatry, neurology]

- ★ An SSRI such as escitalopram is first-line, with talk therapy. SNRIs,
  tricyclics and vortioxetine are alternatives.
- Affects 20% to 60% of stroke patients depending on definition and
  timing; varies with stroke location.
- Why not bupropion: if SSRIs fail or are not tolerated.
- Why not nortriptyline: effective in trials, but tricyclics carry more
  anticholinergic effect.
- Why not transcranial magnetic stimulation: growing evidence, not
  first-line.

## Item 86. Who gets naloxone [opioids]

- ★ Naloxone and overdose education for patients at increased risk: a
  history of substance use disorder or overdose, sleep-disordered
  breathing, 50 MME a day or more, a benzodiazepine with the opioid, or a
  taper with loss of tolerance and risk of return to higher doses (2022
  CDC). (The dose, benzodiazepine and history items are in Foundations,
  fd-opi-04.)
- Why not nightly alprazolam alone, a 3-day low-dose hydrocodone course,
  or hospice morphine as needed: none meets the threshold by itself.

## Item 87. Hypoglycaemia after gastric bypass [gastroenterology, endocrine]

- ★ Sweating, shaking and faintness with a glucose of 50 to 55 one to two
  hours after sugary or dairy drinks, after bypass: late dumping syndrome,
  from rapid carbohydrate absorption and exaggerated insulin release.
- Dumping affects about 40% after bariatric surgery. Early dumping is 30
  to 60 minutes after eating, from fluid shift and gut hormones: pain,
  diarrhoea, bloating, flushing, palpitations, hypotension, syncope, with
  no hypoglycaemia.
- Why not gastroparesis: delayed emptying, no hypoglycaemia, usually
  diabetic.
- Why not idiopathic postprandial syndrome: symptoms without measured
  hypoglycaemia.
- Why not insulinoma: hypoglycaemia regardless of meals.

## Item 88. Acute otitis media with amoxicillin allergy [paediatrics]

- ★ A 10-month-old with a temperature of 39.2 and a bulging drum meets
  the severe criteria for antibiotics; with hives to amoxicillin, cefdinir
  (or cefuroxime, cefpodoxime, ceftriaxone).
- Severe means moderate or severe otalgia, otalgia over 48 hours, or a
  temperature of 39 or more, unilateral or bilateral (SOR B).
- Under 24 months without severe symptoms: antibiotics for bilateral
  disease; older children or unilateral disease may be observed.
- Amoxicillin-clavulanate when amoxicillin was given in the past 30 days,
  with purulent conjunctivitis, or after amoxicillin failure (SOR C).
- Why not ciprofloxacin: not in children. Why not
  sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim: pneumococcal resistance.

## Item 89. A child's fall from a table [paediatrics, emergency]

- ★ Fall from a table, one vomit, a parietal haematoma, consolable, no
  fracture or altered mental status: mild traumatic brain injury.
  Reassurance and outpatient follow-up; no routine imaging.
- CT only for worsening symptoms, signs of headache, or a suspected head
  or neck injury. Skull radiographs have no role.
- Child protection referral is for delayed presentation or inconsistent
  findings or behaviour, none of which is present.

## Item 90. Behavioural help to quit smoking [preventive]

- ★ A smoke-free home and car is a highly effective behavioural
  intervention, part of the Ask, Advise, Assist/Connect model.
- Pharmacotherapy to combine with it: nicotine replacement,
  sustained-release bupropion, varenicline. Combined interventions beat
  either alone.
- Why not caffeine, menthol, hypnotherapy or acupuncture: no role, or no
  moderate-quality evidence.

## Item 91. COPD, GOLD group A [respiratory]

- ★ Initial treatment for group A is a long-acting muscarinic antagonist
  such as umeclidinium; a long-acting beta-agonist is the alternative.
- Group B and group E start on LAMA plus LABA; group E may start on
  triple therapy if eosinophils are 300 or more. (Groups B and E and the
  eosinophil threshold are in Foundations, fd-copd-04 and 05.)

## Item 92. Hyperglycaemic hyperosmolar state [endocrine, acute]

- ★ Glucose 750, osmolality 350, trace ketones, pH 7.3, normal gap,
  hypotensive: hyperosmolar state, not ketoacidosis. Isotonic fluid first.
- Then an insulin infusion at 0.05 units/kg/hour, potassium added after
  the first bolus, bicarbonate only if pH is under 7.0.
- (The osmolality threshold and fluid-first rule are in Foundations,
  fd-dka-02 and 03.)

## Item 93. HFrEF, the fourth pillar [cardiovascular]

- ★ An SGLT2 inhibitor cuts all-cause mortality in HFrEF with or without
  diabetes. (Covered in the Articles set.)
- Why not a calcium channel blocker: no mortality benefit and may worsen
  outcomes.
- Why not a thiazide: adjunct for congestion only.
- Why not a GLP-1 agonist: mortality effect under investigation.
- Why not metformin: the critique says it improves mortality in HFpEF;
  that claim is observational and not used in a question.

## Item 94. Diagnosing diabetes [endocrine]

- ★ With classic symptoms, a random glucose of 200 or more diagnoses
  diabetes on its own. Fasting 126 or more, A1c 6.5% or more, or a 2-hour
  75-g value of 200 or more each need a second abnormal result. (The
  fasting and A1c thresholds are in Foundations, fd-dm-01 and 02.)
- Fasting 100 to 125 or A1c 5.7% to 6.4% is prediabetes.
- A 1-hour 50-g value of 140 or more is a positive first step of two-step
  gestational screening, not a diagnosis.

## Item 95. Complex regional pain syndrome [pain]

- ★ Burning pain with allodynia, temperature asymmetry, oedema and
  weakness after a forearm fracture: complex regional pain syndrome by the
  Budapest criteria, a clinical diagnosis.
- Why not cellulitis: no fever or infection signs.
- Why not peripheral neuropathy: no stocking-glove pattern.
- Why not factitious or functional disorder: the objective findings.

## Item 96. Pelvic inflammatory disease [womens-health]

- ★ Cervical motion, uterine or adnexal tenderness in a woman at risk is
  enough to treat; no further testing is routinely needed.
- Ultrasound when the picture suggests tubo-ovarian abscess (acute
  unilateral pain, an adnexal mass) or appendicitis (unilateral pain). MRI
  if ultrasound shows an abscess; CT for complications above the pelvis.
  Laparoscopy is not initial.
- Why not a repeat pregnancy test in a week: she is acutely ill now.

## Item 97. High-risk TIA, the first day [neurology]

- ★ ABCD2 of 4 or more within 24 hours: load clopidogrel 300 to 600 mg and
  aspirin 50 to 325 mg, then both daily for 21 days, then one agent.
  Ninety-day stroke risk falls from 7.8% to 5.2% versus aspirin alone.
  (The 21 days is in Foundations, fd-str-03.)
- ABCD2 scores age, blood pressure, clinical features, duration and
  diabetes.
- Why not a single agent or a shorter course: no reduction in stroke
  risk.

## Item 98. Hepatitis C with fibrosis [hepatology]

- ★ Treating hepatitis C improves fibrosis, as does bariatric surgery in
  steatohepatitis.
- HIV coinfection accelerates fibrosis; a PrEP conversation is worthwhile
  but PrEP does not improve fibrosis.
- Antibiotic prophylaxis, non-selective beta-blockers and banding have
  their own indications (prior SBP, low ascitic protein with advanced
  disease, significant varices) and do not treat fibrosis. (Those
  indications are in Foundations, fd-cir-05 and 09.)

## Item 99. Pelvic organ prolapse, mildly bothered [womens-health]

- ★ A grade 2 anterior prolapse (within 1 cm above or below the hymen)
  with mild bother: pelvic floor muscle training, which reduces prolapse
  by 1 to 2 cm and improves symptoms.
- Why not oestrogen alone: no clear evidence.
- Why not surgery: the usual ultimate treatment, but incontinence can
  worsen after repair, and she is barely bothered.
- Prolapse generally progresses until menopause and then fluctuates.

## Item 100. Acute limb ischaemia [vascular, emergency]

- ★ Sudden pain, a cold numb mottled pulseless foot: transfer now to an
  emergency department with vascular surgery (SOR C); unfractionated
  heparin on arrival. Muscle survives 4 to 6 hours.
- The six Ps: pain, pallor, poikilothermia, pulselessness, paraesthesia,
  paralysis.
- Why not duplex or CT angiography first: do not delay transfer for
  imaging.
- Why not rivaroxaban: for chronic symptomatic PAD, not the acute event.
- Why not outpatient vascular referral: for chronic symptoms.

## Item 101. Bone stress injury, imaging [musculoskeletal]

- ★ Radiographs first (specific, insensitive); if negative and symptoms
  persist, MRI is definitive: more sensitive and specific than CT or bone
  scan, no radiation.
- Radiographs are often normal in the first 2 to 3 weeks, so a repeat in a
  week is not the answer. Point-of-care ultrasound is an adjunct only.

## Item 102. Chronic urticaria flare on an antihistamine [allergy]

- ★ Increase the second-generation H1 antihistamine, up to four times the
  usual dose.
- Omalizumab if aggressive antihistamine treatment fails. Cyclosporine for
  severe refractory disease after high-dose antihistamines and an H2
  blocker trial. A short course of oral steroid can settle a flare but is
  not for chronic control.
- Why not a pseudoallergen-free diet or a nasal steroid: no good evidence,
  wrong disease.

## Item 103. Acute rhinosinusitis, who gets antibiotics [ENT]

- ★ Double sickening (new symptoms after initial improvement) or severe
  disease (fever of 39 or more with purulent discharge or facial pain)
  means immediate empiric antibiotics.
- Otherwise watchful waiting up to 10 days with symptomatic care. Major
  symptoms: purulent discharge, congestion, facial pain, reduced smell,
  fever. Minor: headache, cough, fatigue, ear pressure, halitosis, tooth
  pain.
- Why not fever of 38, tooth pain or purulent discharge alone: none
  reaches the threshold.

## Item 104. Lung cancer screening, a former smoker [preventive]

- ★ Annual low-dose CT from 50 to 80 with 20 pack-years, currently smoking
  or quit within 15 years. (In Foundations, fd-prev-04.)
- A 44-year-old with 25 pack-years who quit 6 years ago starts at 50 and
  stays eligible until 15 years after quitting, at 53.

## Item 105. Nongonococcal urethritis [infection]

- ★ Dysuria and mucopurulent discharge with a negative gonorrhoea and
  chlamydia NAAT: azithromycin 1 g once. Doxycycline 7 days or
  moxifloxacin 7 to 14 days are the alternatives for Mycoplasma
  genitalium, which causes 15% to 20% of cases with a known cause; half
  have none.
- Diagnostic criteria: typical symptoms or exposure plus 10 or more WBCs
  per high-power field on first-void urine, 2 or more per oil field on a
  discharge Gram stain, or positive leukocyte esterase on first-void urine.
- Without NAAT results: ceftriaxone IM plus doxycycline for 7 days,
  empirically.
- Why not gentamicin plus azithromycin: an alternative gonorrhoea regimen.
- Why not metronidazole: trichomonas, 1% to 8% of male cases, needs a
  molecular test.
- Why not levofloxacin: a non-first-line chlamydia option.

## Item 106. Thunderclap headache with fluctuating consciousness [neurology]

- ★ Non-contrast CT head immediately; neurosurgery if positive. (CT within
  6 hours is in Foundations, fd-ha-02.)
- MRI takes longer and adds nothing for subarachnoid haemorrhage.
- Pain and nausea control wait behind the scan. Lumbar puncture if the CT
  is negative and suspicion stays high, especially beyond 6 hours.

## Item 107. Cannabinoid hyperemesis [gastroenterology]

- ★ Daily cannabis, weeks of vomiting relieved by hot showers:
  cannabinoid hyperemesis. Haloperidol beats metoclopramide,
  prochlorperazine and steroids.
- Topical capsaicin to the upper abdomen works through TRPV1 receptors.
  Benzodiazepines help acutely; tricyclics may help long term. Stopping
  cannabis is the definitive treatment.
- Why not diclofenac gel: no role.

## Item 108. Fibromyalgia, first drug [rheumatology]

- ★ Duloxetine (SOR A). Also level A: amitriptyline, cyclobenzaprine,
  pregabalin, milnacipran (which also helps fatigue and cognition).
  Venlafaxine is less studied.
- Why not acetaminophen or gabapentin: insufficient evidence.
- Why not hydrocodone: no routine role; dependence and hyperalgesia.
- Why not ibuprofen: no better than placebo, with harms.

## Item 109. Nocturnal enuresis, drug choice [paediatrics]

- ★ Behavioural methods and an enuresis alarm are first-line. When a drug
  is wanted, desmopressin, oral or intranasal.
- Imipramine and oxybutynin if desmopressin fails. Tamsulosin has no
  place.

## Item 110. Adult ADHD, stimulant not tolerated [psychiatry]

- ★ Atomoxetine (or viloxazine), the second-line class, when a stimulant
  is not tolerated or inadequate.
- Stimulant adverse effects: headache, insomnia, appetite and weight loss,
  anxiety, depression, raised heart rate and pressure.
- Why not clonidine or guanfacine: approved only for children and
  adolescents; limited adult evidence.
- Why not nortriptyline: limited evidence, not approved. Why not
  escitalopram: ineffective.

## Item 111. First anterior shoulder dislocation in a young athlete [sports]

- ★ Under 25 and active: surgical repair lowers recurrence, which
  otherwise runs up to 90%.
- Non-operative care is a sling for 2 to 4 weeks with graded range of
  motion; return to play when pain-free with symmetric motion and
  sport-specific movements, as early as 2 to 3 weeks.
- Why not an intra-articular steroid: not advised after dislocation.

## Item 112. Inflammatory mastitis [womens-health]

- ★ Three weeks postpartum, hours of a tender red swollen segment, no
  fever: inflammatory mastitis. NSAIDs, cold compresses, watchful waiting.
- Antibiotics (narrow spectrum, staphylococci and streptococci) if no
  better in 24 to 48 hours.
- No longer recommended: warm compresses, frequent pumping to empty the
  breast. Not helpful: CBC and CRP (raised in both inflammatory and
  bacterial), milk culture (skin flora give false positives).

## Item 113. Two cystic fibrosis carriers [genetics]

- ★ Autosomal recessive: each child has a 25% chance of being affected,
  50% of being a carrier, 25% of being neither.

## Item 114. Fatigue after COVID-19 [general]

- ★ Nine months of unrefreshing sleep, new fatigue, post-exertional
  malaise, brain fog and lightheadedness, PHQ-9 of 3: myalgic
  encephalomyelitis / chronic fatigue syndrome by the National Academy of
  Medicine criteria.
- The separating feature: exertion makes it worse. Depression,
  fibromyalgia and lupus fatigue improve with exercise. Sleep apnoea gives
  unrefreshing sleep without profound fatigue or post-exertional malaise.

## Item 115. Diagnosing HFrEF [cardiovascular]

- ★ Echocardiography makes the diagnosis: ejection fraction of 40% or less
  with a raised BNP. (BNP thresholds are in the Articles set.)
- Right-sided symptoms are common but less specific: early satiety,
  bloating, tight clothes, breathlessness bending forward. Signs: raised
  JVP, S3, displaced apex. Prior MI carries a hazard ratio of 3.49.
- Chest radiography is normal in about 20% of acute HFrEF. ECG looks for
  contributors, not the diagnosis. CT angiography is for low-risk
  coronary evaluation, not a patient with a known MI.

## Item 116. Screening a well 3-year-old [paediatrics, preventive]

- ★ USPSTF: vision screening at least once between 3 and 5 for amblyopia
  (B).
- Rated insufficient by USPSTF though recommended by the AAP: anaemia at
  one year, autism at 18 and 24 months. Dental caries screening under 5
  is also insufficient; instead fluoride varnish every 3 to 6 months from
  6 months (B) and supplements where water lacks fluoride (B). Hearing is
  screened at birth, not at 3.

## Item 117. Dermatomyositis at the wellness visit [rheumatology]

- ★ Malignancy: up to 25% are diagnosed with cancer within 3 years of a
  diagnosis after 40. Ovary, lung, pancreas, stomach, colon,
  non-Hodgkin lymphoma. Population screening is not enough; CT or PET by
  individual risk.
- Why not kidney disease or obesity: no increased risk observed.

## Item 118. Cognitive biases [practice]

- ★ Anchoring: favouring data that supports the first impression.
  Availability: a diagnosis seems likely because examples come easily.
  Implicit: unconscious attitudes about race, age, gender and the like.
  Outcome bias: judging decisions by their results.

## Item 119. Heavy menstrual bleeding in an adolescent [womens-health]

- ★ Test for a bleeding disorder first: 20% of adolescents with heavy
  bleeding have one (versus 1% to 2% of the population), mostly platelet
  dysfunction and von Willebrand disease.
- Triggers: a pad or tampon every 1 to 2 hours, bleeding over 7 days,
  clots over an inch, treated anaemia, bleeding with extraction or surgery,
  easy bruising, family history.
- Work-up: CBC, PT, PTT, INR, von Willebrand panel, hCG, ferritin,
  fibrinogen, TSH; STI tests if sexually active; androgens and prolactin
  only with endocrine signs.
- Why not FSH, LH, oestrogen, progesterone: unhelpful. Why not imaging:
  only if initial treatment fails.

## Item 120. Dyspepsia without alarm features [gastroenterology]

- ★ Test and treat for Helicobacter pylori by stool antigen or urea breath
  test; benefit persists at a year.
- Why not serology: lower sensitivity and specificity. Why not
  sucralfate: ineffective. Why not ultrasound: for liver and gallbladder.
- Endoscopy for new dyspepsia over 60, or under 60 with multiple or severe
  alarm features: marked weight loss, progressive dysphagia, an
  epigastric mass, supraclavicular nodes.

## Item 121. Confirmed hypercalcaemia [endocrine]

- ★ After confirmation with ionised or corrected calcium, the first test
  is parathyroid hormone. Primary hyperparathyroidism is the commonest
  cause; malignancy must be excluded.
- Raised PTH: then 25-hydroxyvitamin D, creatinine, 24-hour urine calcium
  and bone density. Parathyroid imaging is for surgical planning, not
  diagnosis. Low PTH points to malignancy and may call for CT.
- History: stones, fractures, mood, lithium, family history of MEN.

## Item 122. Facial weakness that spares the forehead [neurology]

- ★ Forehead sparing means a central (supranuclear) lesion, because the
  upper face receives bilateral cortical input. MRI of the brain, not Bell
  palsy treatment.
- Bell palsy affects the whole side including the forehead; prednisone
  with valacyclovir is its treatment. Plasmapheresis and decompression
  have no place in either.

## Item 123. Head injury in a 70-year-old [emergency, geriatrics]

- ★ Over 65 with loss of consciousness, vomiting, amnesia or prior head
  trauma: CT head. Age over 65 is itself a risk factor, and older
  patients bleed with minor mechanisms and normal examinations.
- NSAIDs are avoided after traumatic brain injury for bleeding risk.

## Item 124. Dehydration in a 3-day-old [paediatrics]

- ★ Capillary refill time is the most reliable sign; over 2 seconds
  strongly predicts dehydration.
- Urine output is sensitive but not specific. Dry mucosa is subjective.
  Skin turgor is unreliable in neonates because their skin is elastic. A
  sunken fontanelle depends on position.

## Item 125. Adolescent depression, approved drugs [psychiatry]

- ★ Fluoxetine and escitalopram are FDA-approved for adolescent
  depression. Sertraline, bupropion and venlafaxine are used off-label.
- Esketamine nasal spray is for treatment-resistant depression in adults,
  with an oral antidepressant.

## Item 126. Acute bronchitis [respiratory]

- ★ Two weeks of productive cough, afebrile, saturating normally, rhonchi
  that clear: acute bronchitis, viral in 90%, lasting 2 to 3 weeks.
  Dextromethorphan, guaifenesin and honey reduce cough frequency (SOR B).
- Antibiotics shorten the cough by 0.46 days with net harm (SOR A).
  Albuterol only if wheezing (SOR B). Codeine has no benefit and real
  side effects.

## Item 127. Ottawa ankle rules [musculoskeletal]

- ★ Radiograph for bone tenderness at the posterior edge or tip of either
  malleolus, at the base of the fifth metatarsal or the navicular, or
  inability to bear weight for four steps.
- The anterior edge of a malleolus, the fourth metatarsal, and swelling
  do not count.

## Item 128. Functional constipation in a teenager [paediatrics]

- ★ Rome IV: two or fewer stools a week, painful hard stools, stools that
  clog the toilet (also retentive posturing, incontinence, a rectal mass).
  Polyethylene glycol daily has good evidence.
- Probiotics have none. Enterography and colonoscopy are for red flags,
  which are absent with steady growth, normal labs and no blood.

## Item 129. Statin intensity in diabetes [cardiovascular]

- ★ Diabetes at 40 to 75 with LDL of 70 or more: a moderate-intensity
  statin without a risk calculation; high intensity for elevated risk,
  such as multiple risk factors or age 50 to 75. A 45-year-old smoker with
  triglycerides of 300 gets atorvastatin 40 to 80. (Intensities and the
  diabetes rule are in Foundations, fd-lip-03 and 12.)
- Pravastatin 20 is low intensity; simvastatin 20 to 40 is moderate and
  is not used above 40. Ezetimibe and fenofibrate are not first-line.

## Item 130. Tarsal tunnel syndrome [musculoskeletal]

- ★ Paraesthesia and burning in the sole, pain behind the medial
  malleolus, a positive Tinel sign at the ankle, a year after a sprain:
  tibial nerve entrapment under the flexor retinaculum. No gold-standard
  test; electrophysiology helps, MRI if a mass is suspected.
- Plantar fasciitis: pain with the first morning steps, tender at the
  medial calcaneal tuberosity. Achilles tendinopathy: posterior heel at the
  insertion, worse with activity. Haglund deformity: upper posterior
  calcaneus, ill-fitting shoes. Heel pad syndrome: a bruise-like central
  heel pain, reproducible by palpation.

## Item 131. Firearm range safety [preventive]

- ★ Electronic hearing protection: gunshots exceed 140 dB and one shot can
  cause permanent loss; electronic muffs amplify quiet sounds such as
  range commands while blocking the shot.
- Noise-cancelling headphones are inadequate. Loose clothing traps hot
  brass. Regular glasses are not safety glasses.

## Item 132. Chest pain in the office, the ECG [cardiovascular]

- ★ ST changes, new left bundle branch block, Q waves and new T-wave
  inversions raise the likelihood of acute coronary syndrome and mean
  immediate transport.
- First-degree block, frequent PVCs, sinus tachycardia and LVH voltage do
  not. About 1% of office visits are for chest pain, and 2% to 4% of
  those are unstable angina or infarction.

## Item 133. Osteopenia with vertebral fractures [musculoskeletal]

- ★ A fragility fracture (no trauma, or a fall from standing height or
  less) makes the diagnosis osteoporosis whatever the T-score, and the
  patient is treated. (Treatment indications are in Foundations,
  fd-ost-03.)
- T-score minus 1.0 to minus 2.49 is osteopenia; minus 2.5 or lower is
  osteoporosis.
- Spondylolisthesis: anterior slip of one vertebra on another,
  degenerative. Spondylolysis: a pars stress fracture in young athletes.

## Item 134. Second-trimester bleeding with no prenatal labs [obstetrics]

- ★ Blood type and Rh first: an Rh-negative woman needs anti-D
  immunoglobulin, and the risk of fetomaternal mixing is high.
- Antiphospholipid testing is for loss after 10 weeks, recurrent early
  loss, certain preterm deliveries, or thrombosis. HIV, toxoplasma and a
  cocaine screen each have their place but are not time-critical.

## Item 135. Needlestick from a hepatitis B source [infection]

- ★ Vaccinated with surface antibody over 10 mIU/mL: immune, nothing
  needed. Immunity can also come from past infection (core and surface
  antibodies both reactive).
- Negative serology: immune globulin, with the vaccine series.

## Item 136. A healthy 20-year-old with a BMI of 31 [preventive]

- ★ HIV screening for everyone 15 to 65 (A). (In Foundations, fd-prev-08.)
  Also hepatitis C.
- Cervical screening is recommended against before 21 (D). Chlamydia is
  for sexually active women under 25 (B). Diabetes screening is 35 to 70
  with overweight (B); below 35 she gets intensive behavioural weight
  intervention. Skin cancer: counselling on UV exposure for fair skin
  from 6 months to 24 years (B), but no screening examination (I).

## Item 137. Flashes, floaters and a field defect [ophthalmology]

- ★ Retinal detachment: urgent ophthalmology with a dilated examination.
- Migraine aura is transient and followed by headache, and new at 65 is
  unusual. Central retinal artery occlusion is sudden painless loss with
  an afferent pupillary defect and a cherry-red spot, in a vascular
  patient. Globe rupture needs trauma. Stroke does not give flashes and
  floaters.

## Item 138. Low back pain, when to image [musculoskeletal]

- ★ New urinary retention suggests cauda equina and is an indication for
  advanced imaging; so are bladder fullness and saddle anaesthesia.
- Early imaging raises surgery, opioid use, cost, pain scores and
  absenteeism. Smoking and intravenous drug use raise cancer and abscess
  risk but are not indications alone. Pain severity, posture and
  nocturnal pain do not correlate with serious pathology, though pain in
  the second half of the night deserves a second look.

## Item 139. Intranasal naloxone [opioids]

- ★ Peak plasma concentration at 15 to 30 minutes, though the effect can
  show sooner. Half-life 2 hours, shorter than many long-acting opioids,
  so monitoring continues after reversal.

## Item 140. Inguinal hernia [surgery]

- ★ A bulge that appears on straining is an inguinal hernia and needs no
  imaging; nearly half of imaging referrals are unnecessary. Symptomatic
  or limiting function: surgical referral. Asymptomatic: watchful
  waiting. Physiotherapy has no role.

## Item 141. Normal-pressure hydrocephalus [neurology]

- ★ Gait change, urinary incontinence and dementia with enlarged
  ventricles, gait improving after high-volume lumbar puncture:
  ventriculoperitoneal shunt.
- Donepezil (Alzheimer), carbidopa-levodopa (Lewy body and Parkinson),
  levothyroxine (hypothyroidism) and surgery with chemotherapy (tumour)
  treat other dementias.

## Item 142. Gross haematuria at 65 [genitourinary]

- ★ Every adult with gross haematuria is referred for cystoscopy and
  imaging; the positive predictive value for malignancy in older men is
  high, and cystoscopy is 87% to 100% sensitive for bladder cancer.
- Not a repeat urinalysis in a month, not empiric antibiotics without
  infection, and not cytology, which is too insensitive to replace
  cystoscopy.

## Item 143. Counselling the ambivalent smoker [practice]

- ★ Open questions, affirmations, reflective listening and summaries
  (OARS) are motivational interviewing, with level A evidence for weight,
  blood pressure and alcohol in primary care.
- BATHE structures psychosocial problems with targeted questions. The
  five A's are a stepwise, directive guide. The transtheoretical model
  identifies the stage of readiness (precontemplation to maintenance) and
  can set up motivational interviewing.

## Item 144. A leg lesion in type 1 diabetes [dermatology]

- ★ A dusky red sharply bordered nodule on the shin that enlarges,
  flattens and develops a yellow-brown centre: necrobiosis lipoidica.
- Diabetic dermopathy is crops of brown atrophic patches. Lipoatrophy is
  depressed but otherwise normal skin. Rubeosis is facial and limb
  reddening. Eruptive xanthomas are clustered small red-yellow papules on
  extensors and buttocks.

## Item 145. Metformin and vitamin B12 [endocrine]

- ★ Metformin lowers B12 (SOR A): a trial showed a 19% fall in levels and
  a 7% absolute rise in deficiency against placebo.
- Chromatopsia belongs to sildenafil. Metformin is contraindicated in
  renal impairment but does not cause it, and it does not cause weight
  gain or ileus.

## Item 146. Pneumonia in a well 3-year-old [paediatrics, infection]

- ★ Focal crackles and lobar consolidation point to a typical pathogen
  (pneumococcus, Haemophilus): amoxicillin for 7 days. Most childhood
  pneumonia is viral.
- Azithromycin is for atypical pneumonia. Cefdinir works but is too broad
  for first-line. Doxycycline is not for a 3-year-old.
  Sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim is not a pneumonia drug.

## Item 147. Dorsal PIP dislocation [musculoskeletal]

- ★ Neurovascularly intact, a small fracture under 20% of the joint
  surface: reduce in the office, splint at about 30 degrees of flexion for
  2 to 4 weeks, buddy tape, radiograph after reduction and at a week.
- Surgical referral for irreducible or unstable injuries, over 30% of the
  articular surface, angulation over 15 degrees, malrotation,
  comminution, persistent subluxation, open injuries, vascular compromise
  or loss of function. MRI and physiotherapy have no role here.

## Item 148. Giardia after travel [infection]

- ★ Greasy diarrhoea, cramping and nausea 1 to 2 weeks after exposure,
  fever and vomiting uncommon: metronidazole, nitazoxanide or tinidazole,
  given to symptomatic patients to relieve symptoms and stop spread.
- Azithromycin, ciprofloxacin and ivermectin treat other causes of
  traveller's diarrhoea.

## Item 149. Depression with epilepsy [psychiatry]

- ★ Bupropion, trazodone and tricyclics raise seizure risk and are
  avoided. (Bupropion specifically is in Foundations, fd-dep-07.) SSRIs,
  SNRIs, mirtazapine and vortioxetine are preferred.

## Item 150. A healthy 20-year-old woman [preventive]

- ★ Hepatitis C screening from 18 to 79 (B). (In Foundations,
  fd-prev-08.)
- Cervical screening is recommended against before 21. Lipid screening at
  this age has uncertain evidence. Skin cancer examination is I; UV
  counselling for fair skin to 24 is B.

## Item 151. Hypertensive emergency, who gets lowered fast [cardiovascular]

- ★ Acute aortic dissection: systolic to 100 to 120 within 20 minutes and
  heart rate under 60, to cut shear stress and stop propagation.
- Ischaemic stroke, intracerebral haemorrhage, hypertensive
  encephalopathy and renal failure are lowered cautiously.

## Item 152. Daytime sleepiness in an obese snorer [respiratory]

- ★ Obesity, snoring and fatigue: polysomnography is the definitive test.
- TSH would fit if dry skin, constipation or mood change were present;
  B12, folate, iron and a metabolic panel each need their own pointers,
  which are absent.

## Item 153. Screening a 16-year-old [preventive]

- ★ Anxiety screening from 8 to 18 (B). Chlamydia and gonorrhoea only if
  sexually active (B). Eating disorders, blood pressure and scoliosis are
  all I.

## Item 154. Adhesive capsulitis on examination [musculoskeletal]

- ★ Restricted passive range, especially external rotation, flexion and
  abduction, with normal radiographs. Glenohumeral osteoarthritis also
  limits passive motion but shows on the film.
- Restricted active with preserved passive range is extra-articular:
  rotator cuff tendinopathy, impingement, bursitis, biceps tendinitis,
  cuff tears. Weakness on resisted testing suggests a full-thickness
  tear. Apprehension in abduction and external rotation is instability.
  Crepitus is nonspecific.

## Item 155. Diabetic foot ulcer, probe to bone [infection]

- ★ Initial evaluation for osteomyelitis: probe-to-bone, ESR, and plain
  radiography, which is cheap, safe and may make advanced imaging
  unnecessary. (Probe-to-bone meaning is in Foundations, fd-ssti-09.)
- WBC grades severity but is normal in about half of severe infections. A
  highly raised ESR suggests osteomyelitis. Superficial swabs are poor;
  deep tissue cultures are used. Oximetry assesses perfusion, not
  infection.

## Item 156. Bilateral leg swelling on oestrogen [cardiovascular]

- ★ Chronic bilateral pitting oedema with no heart failure signs in a
  woman on oestrogen: hold the oestrogen and watch. Her age also argues
  for stopping it.
- ACE inhibitors do not cause oedema and can reduce calcium channel
  blocker oedema. Ankle-brachial indices are for suspected arterial
  disease, and are done before compression for venous disease.
  Compression ultrasound is for suspected DVT, which is acute and
  unilateral; a low Wells score with a negative D-dimer excludes it.
  Duplex with reflux assesses chronic venous insufficiency.

## Item 157. Syphilis in pregnancy with penicillin allergy [infection]

- ★ Desensitise and give benzathine penicillin G; it is the only treatment
  proven to cure the mother and protect the fetus.
- Azithromycin and erythromycin fail both. Ceftriaxone lacks data.
  Doxycycline and tetracycline are not used in the second and third
  trimesters.

## Item 158. Lifestyle advice for a child above the 80th centile [paediatrics]

- ★ Sixty minutes a day of moderate to vigorous activity from 6 to 17.
  Five servings of fruit and vegetables; no fruit juice as a substitute.
- Calorie restriction works only with activity and behavioural change.
  Screen-based physical activity (movement games) can help.

## Item 159. Symmetric foot numbness with normal labs [neurology]

- ★ Symmetric distal paraesthesia with normal reflexes, strength, pulses
  and screening labs: nerve conduction studies and EMG next.
- Lyme serology and vitamin testing follow if the pattern is axonal.
  Gabapentin treats pain, not the question. MRI is for central signs or
  asymmetry.

## Item 160. Achilles tendinopathy [musculoskeletal]

- ★ A clinical diagnosis; physiotherapy for activity modification and
  loading exercises. No imaging unless another diagnosis is in question.
- Complete rest and corticosteroid injection are not recommended.
  Platelet-rich plasma is not first-line and may not beat placebo.

## Item 161. The anxious new diabetic [practice]

- ★ Motivational interviewing: find what matters to the patient and build
  the impetus from it. Scare tactics do not work, jargon obstructs, and a
  pile of written instructions overwhelms.

## Item 162. Adolescent diarrhoea with falling centiles [gastroenterology]

- ★ Diarrhoea, pain, weight loss and a fall across height and weight
  centiles: screen for coeliac disease with IgA tissue transglutaminase
  (95% sensitive and specific), with a total IgA because 2% to 3% are IgA
  deficient.
- Endoscopy with biopsy confirms a positive test; some groups allow a
  no-biopsy diagnosis in children with very high titres. Faecal fat starts
  as a single qualitative sample. Breath testing is for bacterial
  overgrowth, which needs abnormal anatomy or motility and presents with
  bloating.

## Item 163. Suspected testosterone deficiency [endocrine]

- ★ Loss of axillary and pubic hair is among the more specific symptoms.
  Fatigue, low libido and weak stamina are common and nonspecific.
- Memory trouble has many causes. Temporal thinning is androgenic
  alopecia; eyebrow loss suggests thyroid disease.

## Item 164. A continuous murmur in a well 3-year-old [paediatrics]

- ★ A soft continuous infraclavicular murmur heard upright, changing with
  head movement and gone supine: a venous hum. Reassure; no follow-up.

## Item 165. Anaphylaxis needing two doses of epinephrine [emergency]

- ★ Refractory anaphylaxis goes to the emergency department and is
  observed 6 to 12 hours for a biphasic reaction, even if she feels well.
- H1 and H2 blockers and steroids are adjuncts after the fact, not the
  treatment.

## Item 166. Bridging a mechanical valve for colonoscopy [haematology]

- ★ Mechanical aortic valve, prior embolic stroke and DVT, GFR 28,
  high-bleeding-risk procedure: hold warfarin and bridge with an
  intravenous heparin infusion to a therapeutic aPTT or anti-Xa.
- INR goal with this valve and history is 2.5 to 3.5. Vitamin K is not
  needed when stable and not bleeding. Enoxaparin is out at that GFR.
  Heparin 5000 units subcutaneously is a prophylactic dose.

## Item 167. Buckle fracture of the distal radius [paediatrics]

- ★ Splint, soft cast or wrap for 3 weeks; a rigid cast is unnecessary.
  Repeat radiographs only if pain or tenderness persists (Choosing
  Wisely).
- A buckle fracture is an incomplete compression fracture with the cortex
  intact, common because children's bones are elastic.

## Item 168. Screening a 19-year-old [preventive]

- ★ Unhealthy drug use screening from 18 (B): nonprescribed use of
  psychoactive drugs or illegally obtained drugs.
- Dental disease, dyslipidaemia, skin cancer and suicide risk at this age
  are all I.

## Item 169. Milky-white watery diarrhoea in an oyster fisherman [infection]

- ★ Large-volume rice-water or milky stools: Vibrio, and tested for
  regardless of shellfish or brackish water exposure.
- IDSA testing for C. difficile, enteropathogenic and enterotoxigenic E.
  coli and Yersinia is for diarrhoea with fever, blood or mucus, sepsis
  signs, or severe cramping or tenderness.

## Item 170. Health maintenance in Down syndrome [preventive]

- ★ TSH every 1 to 2 years from 21; hypothyroidism affects about half of
  adults over 30 with Down syndrome.
- Dementia screening from 40 with a validated tool; rare before 40.
  Cardiovascular prevention per USPSTF; lipids not due after 2 years. No
  DXA before 65. No screening cervical radiographs for atlantoaxial
  instability; watch for myelopathy (incontinence, gait change, brisk
  reflexes, clonus).

## Item 171. Breathless at 9600 feet [emergency]

- ★ Progressive dyspnoea at rest, tachycardia and frothy sputum on day 3
  at altitude: high-altitude pulmonary oedema. Oxygen and descend.
- Acute mountain sickness risk rises sleeping above about 8200 feet.
  Acetazolamide prevents it but does not treat HAPE. Dexamethasone is
  adjunctive for cerebral oedema. Nifedipine prevents HAPE and treats it
  only when descent or a hyperbaric chamber is impossible.

## Item 172. Antibiotics and Stevens-Johnson syndrome [dermatology]

- ★ Sulfonamides, such as sulfamethoxazole-trimethoprim, cause most
  antibiotic-associated Stevens-Johnson syndrome; antibiotics cause over a
  quarter of cases.

## Item 173. One episode of postmenopausal spotting [womens-health]

- ★ Transvaginal ultrasound or endometrial biopsy are both acceptable
  first tests. A stripe of 4 mm or less has over 99% negative predictive
  value for cancer: no further evaluation unless bleeding recurs.
- Recurrent or persistent bleeding: tissue sampling; then hysteroscopy
  with curettage if biopsy fails to explain it.

## Item 174. Catching and popping after a fall on the arm [musculoskeletal]

- ★ A catching or popping sensation with a positive anterior slide test:
  glenoid labral tear (positive likelihood ratio 6.0).
- Rotator cuff tear: pain and weakness overhead, positive empty-can and
  external rotation tests. Acromioclavicular arthritis: superior pain,
  tender joint, positive cross-body adduction. Adhesive capsulitis:
  restricted passive range. Osteoarthritis: gradual, older patients.

## Item 175. A positive alcohol screen [preventive]

- ★ Brief counselling intervention for every adult screening positive for
  unhealthy alcohol use (B); SBIRT reduces risky drinking, and
  motivational interviewing is the usual form.
- CBT, mindfulness and interpersonal therapy are longer-term treatments.
  DSM-5 alcohol use disorder is referred for treatment.

## Item 176. Anal fissure [gastroenterology]

- ★ With fluids, fibre and sitz baths, a topical sphincter relaxant 2 to 4
  times a day; topical diltiazem is best tolerated.
- Topical nitroglycerin works but headache and lightheadedness stop one in
  five. Botulinum toxin also works. Capsaicin is an irritant; oral
  calcium channel blockers do nothing.

## Item 177. Preventing diverticulitis [gastroenterology]

- ★ A vegetarian diet lowers risk. Risk factors: constipation, low fibre,
  age, red meat, smoking, obesity, inactivity, aspirin and NSAIDs,
  genetics. Nuts, seeds and popcorn are not risk factors (SOR B).
- Left lower quadrant tenderness is the most specific finding. Work-up:
  CBC, metabolic panel, CRP, urinalysis; CT with contrast if uncertain.
  Treatment by severity; surgery for abscess, peritonitis or recurrence
  prevention. No screening.

## Item 178. Trigeminal neuralgia [neurology]

- ★ Electric-shock pain in the cheek, lip and chin triggered by touch or
  eating: cranial nerve V. VII moves the face, X swallows and phonates,
  XI turns the neck, XII moves the tongue.

## Item 179. Social determinants and dementia [geriatrics]

- ★ Social engagement protects against Alzheimer disease and related
  dementias. Risk: falling BMI in old age, early-life adversity, low
  education, food insecurity, midlife obesity, manual labour, low
  socioeconomic status.

## Item 180. Proximal aching and stiffness at 72 [rheumatology]

- ★ Proximal pain and stiffness, mild weakness, mild anaemia, ESR 88, CRP
  high, CK normal, ANA negative: polymyalgia rheumatica. Clinical
  diagnosis; oral glucocorticoids with rapid improvement, or rethink.
- Giant cell arteritis travels with it: temporal headache, visual change,
  constitutional symptoms; treat early to save vision.
- Fibromyalgia has normal inflammatory markers. Polymyositis and statin
  myopathy raise CK. Lupus almost always has a positive ANA.

## Item 181. Screening for herpes simplex [preventive]

- ★ No serologic screening of asymptomatic adolescents, adults or pregnant
  patients (D): false positives are common and asymptomatic positives are
  not treated.

## Item 182. Red currant jelly stool in a 2-year-old [paediatrics]

- ★ Intermittent pain, vomiting and blood-tinged mucous stool:
  intussusception, usually ileocolic, in infants and toddlers.
  Ultrasound first; an air or contrast enema confirms and reduces it;
  surgery if the enema fails.
- Necrotising enterocolitis is a sick neonate with unstable vitals.
  Pyloric stenosis vomits projectile without blood. Volvulus distends
  without bloody stool.

## Item 183. Quitting after a relapse [preventive]

- ★ Seven approved drugs: bupropion, varenicline, five forms of nicotine
  replacement. Varenicline is the most effective single drug (NNT 7).
  Combinations beat monotherapy, and varenicline with nicotine
  replacement beats bupropion with nicotine replacement.

## Item 184. Recurrent calcium stones despite lifestyle [renal]

- ★ A high-dose thiazide; allopurinol and potassium citrate are the other
  options. Furosemide promotes stones. Magnesium oxide does not prevent
  them. Tamsulosin helps passage, not prevention.
- Lifestyle: 2.5 to 3 L of fluid, high fibre, low animal protein, sodium
  under 4 to 5 g, calcium kept moderate at 1200 mg rather than restricted.

## Item 185. Confused with a sodium of 118 [renal, acute]

- ★ Symptomatic severe hyponatraemia (under 125): a 100 mL bolus of 3%
  saline, repeatable twice at 10-minute intervals, to raise sodium 4 to 6
  mEq/L; sodium checked after each bolus and every 6 hours for a day. (The
  bolus itself is in Foundations, fd-hna-06.)
- Limit correction to 8 to 12 mEq/L in 24 hours or 18 in 48 to avoid
  osmotic demyelination. Normal saline is for non-severe hypovolaemic
  hyponatraemia (125 to 134). Desmopressin treats diabetes insipidus, and
  with 5% dextrose reverses overcorrection.

## Item 186. Acute lumbar radiculopathy [musculoskeletal]

- ★ No red flags: conservative care; most improve within 2 weeks.
  Mobilisation and manipulation have level B evidence. Stay as active as
  pain allows; bed rest is out. Steroids, NSAIDs and muscle relaxants
  lack evidence. MRI if no improvement at 6 weeks.

## Item 187. Stage 2 hypertension off treatment [cardiovascular]

- ★ 162/100 confirmed is stage 2 (160 or 100, or 20/10 over goal): start
  two drugs, a calcium channel blocker with an ACE inhibitor or ARB.
  (Starting two drugs for stage 2 is in Foundations, fd-htn-06.)
- Beta-blockers are not first-line without a comorbidity such as coronary
  disease or heart failure. Aldosterone antagonists are for resistant
  hypertension. The critique states that race-based prescribing of
  thiazides or calcium channel blockers for Black patients is falling out
  of favour; Foundations fd-htn-03 still teaches it. Flagged for the user.

## Item 188. Anorexia nervosa, who is admitted [psychiatry]

- ★ Severe electrolyte disturbance, such as potassium 2.6, needs inpatient
  care for cardiac risk. Hair loss, muscle atrophy, parotid enlargement
  and mild hypernatraemia occur but do not force admission.

## Item 189. Lynch syndrome at 30 [gastroenterology]

- ★ Colonoscopy from 20 to 25, repeated every 1 to 2 years. Stool tests
  and sigmoidoscopy are inadequate. Average risk starts at 45.
- Also raised: endometrial, ovarian, stomach, small bowel, pancreatic
  cancer.

## Item 190. Feeding in acute pancreatitis [gastroenterology]

- ★ Early enteral feeding within 24 hours, as a low-fat solid diet;
  liquids and soft diets have no advantage and solid diets may shorten
  stay. Enteral feeding improves gut perfusion and barrier function and
  beats parenteral nutrition on mortality, organ failure and infection.
- Not tolerating oral intake by 72 hours: nasogastric or nasojejunal
  feeding. Parenteral nutrition only when tube feeding fails.
  Low-carbohydrate diets are high in fat and avoided.

## Item 191. Osteoporosis screening at 57 [preventive]

- ★ DXA at 65 for all women; earlier for postmenopausal women with a risk
  factor such as smoking, excessive alcohol or a parental hip fracture.
  (The age is in Foundations, fd-ost-01.)
- FRAX counsels on future fracture risk; it does not decide who gets DXA.
  A mother on alendronate without a fracture is not a risk factor.

## Item 192. ECG in hyperkalaemia [renal, cardiovascular]

- ★ Hyperkalaemia: peaked T waves, prolonged PR, loss of P waves,
  prolonged QRS; a wide QRS, bradycardia or junctional rhythm predicts
  ventricular fibrillation and death within hours. (Peaked T waves and
  the calcium response are in Foundations, fd-hk-02 and 03.)
- Hypokalaemia: flattened or inverted T waves, prolonged QT, ST
  depression, U waves. Delta waves are Wolff-Parkinson-White.

## Item 193. A loose front tooth after a baseball [emergency, dental]

- ★ Reposition the tooth in its socket and have him bite on gauze;
  reimplantation after an hour rarely succeeds. Do not remove a loose
  tooth; the socket is the best medium. An avulsed tooth is held by the
  crown, rinsed with saline and reimplanted, or carried in saline or
  milk. Antibiotics and imaging are not needed without a fracture.

## Item 194. Graves disease [endocrine]

- ★ Diffuse high radioiodine uptake is Graves disease; methimazole is the
  antithyroid drug of choice for its safety; propylthiouracil carries
  severe liver injury risk and is kept for the first trimester.
  Radioiodine ablation and thyroidectomy are the other options.
- Uptake in one spot is a toxic adenoma, several spots a toxic
  multinodular goitre, low uptake thyroiditis. Prednisone is for thyroid
  storm; verapamil stands in when beta-blockers cannot be used.

## Item 195. Delirium that redirection cannot settle [geriatrics]

- ★ Once reversible causes are excluded, a second-generation antipsychotic
  such as olanzapine is preferred. Haloperidol is less preferred for QT
  prolongation and other effects in the elderly. Benzodiazepines worsen
  delirium in older adults. Lithium and opioids have no role.

## Item 196. Capacity for surgery in a confused 84-year-old [ethics]

- ★ A patient who clearly cannot articulate risks and benefits lacks
  capacity: document it and consult the durable power of attorney.
  Structured tools are for uncertain cases. Psychiatry is rarely needed.
  The treating clinician determines capacity, not the court.

## Item 197. A lost skier, conscious but not shivering [emergency]

- ★ Not shivering, not moving, conscious but not alert: moderate
  hypothermia. Keep horizontal, large heat packs in the axillae and
  around the chest, wrap in an insulated vapour barrier, transport to a
  hospital with an ICU.
- Core temperature is impractical in the field. Warm drinks and food are
  for cold stress or mild hypothermia. Wet clothes stay on unless there
  is shelter and rescue is over 30 minutes away. Intubation is not needed
  and does not warm.

## Item 198. A first raised PSA at 63 [preventive]

- ★ Repeat the PSA in 4 to 8 weeks before biomarkers, imaging or biopsy;
  up to 40% normalise. The age-adjusted upper limit for his sixties is
  4.5. No empiric antibiotics (Choosing Wisely). Digital rectal
  examination is not a screening test alone or with PSA.
- AUA screens 45 to 50 by risk and 50 to 69 every 2 to 4 years; AAFP does
  not recommend routine PSA screening.

## Item 199. Acute watery diarrhoea in a non-toxic adult [infection]

- ★ No testing. Faecal calprotectin, lactoferrin and leukocytes have
  limited value; occult blood and C. difficile testing are not
  recommended either.
- Test when: severe pain or tenderness, blood or mucus, sepsis, fever of
  38.3 or more, immunocompromise, recent hospitalisation, recent
  antibiotics, or diarrhoea beyond 7 days.

## Item 200. Closing a dog bite [emergency]

- ★ A linear facial wound may be closed primarily for cosmesis. Crush
  injuries, hand wounds and punctures heal by secondary intention.
- Delayed presentation raises infection risk: over 6 to 12 hours for a
  limb, 12 to 24 for the face. Immunosuppression, including diabetes,
  raises it too.
