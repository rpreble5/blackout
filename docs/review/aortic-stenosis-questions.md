## Aortic stenosis: recognising and grading it (15 questions)

- **as-dx-01** (single, d1, 4 options) Which examination finding most raises the likelihood of aortic stenosis?
  → An inaudible second heart sound
- **as-dx-02** (multi, d2, 5 options) Which coexisting conditions make it hard to tell whether symptoms come from the aortic stenosis?
  → Atrial fibrillation + Coronary artery disease
- **as-dx-03** (single, d2, 4 options) A 74-year-old has a new systolic murmur and fatigue. What is the preferred first test?
  → Transthoracic echocardiography
- **as-dx-04** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) Above which maximum transaortic velocity is aortic stenosis graded severe?
  → 4 m/s
- **as-dx-05** (single, d3, 4 options) An echo shows valve area 0.8 cm², Vmax 3.4 m/s and mean gradient 28 mm Hg. How is this classified?
  → Low-flow, low-gradient severe stenosis
- **as-dx-06** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) Compared with echocardiography, what is the sensitivity of auscultation for significant valvular disease?
  → About 44%
- **as-dx-07** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) A patient with a bicuspid aortic valve asks about the long-term outlook. What proportion eventually need valve replacement?
  → More than half
- **as-dx-08** (single, d2, 4 options) A patient with aortic stenosis has gastrointestinal bleeding with no obvious source. Which syndrome should be considered?
  → Heyde syndrome
- **as-dx-09** (single, d2, 4 options) Why do patients who have both aortic stenosis and atrial fibrillation get valve replacement less often?
  → Their symptoms are attributed to the arrhythmia
- **as-dx-10** (single, d1, 4 options) What is the characteristic murmur of aortic stenosis?
  → Loud, late-peaking systolic murmur at the right upper sternal border, radiating to the carotids
- **as-dx-11** (single, d2, 4 options) What distinguishes aortic sclerosis from aortic stenosis on echo?
  → Calcification without restricted leaflet movement
- **as-dx-12** (single, d3, 4 options, fixed order) Asymptomatic severe aortic stenosis, ejection fraction 60%, Vmax 4.3 m/s, no surgery planned. How soon should the echo be repeated?
  → In 6 to 12 months
- **as-dx-13** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) What is the estimated two-year mortality of untreated symptomatic severe aortic stenosis?
  → About 50%
- **as-dx-14** (multi, d3, 6 options) Which early or atypical symptoms of aortic stenosis are commonly attributed to aging instead?
  → Fatigue + Exercise intolerance + Lightheadedness
- **as-dx-15** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) Below which aortic valve area is stenosis graded severe?
  → 1.0 cm²

## Aortic stenosis: managing it and replacing the valve (15 questions)

- **as-mx-02** (single, d2, 4 options) Why is a statin still recommended for many patients with aortic stenosis?
  → To reduce cardiovascular events by ten-year atherosclerotic risk, not to slow the stenosis
- **as-mx-03** (single, d2, 4 options) How should antihypertensive treatment be started in a patient with aortic stenosis?
  → Start low and titrate slowly, to avoid vasodilation causing presyncope or syncope
- **as-mx-04** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) An asymptomatic patient has moderate aortic stenosis. How often should echo be repeated?
  → Every 1 to 2 years
- **as-mx-05** (multi, d3, 7 options) Which clinical changes should trigger a repeat echo before the scheduled interval?
  → New symptoms + A louder murmur + A later-peaking murmur + A newly absent second heart sound
- **as-mx-06** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) What activity advice fits moderate aortic stenosis?
  → Low to moderate intensity activity
- **as-mx-07** (single, d1, 4 options) Which patients with aortic stenosis should be referred to cardiology?
  → All with severe stenosis; consider referral for moderate
- **as-mx-08** (multi, d3, 8 options) An asymptomatic patient has severe aortic stenosis. Which findings are indications for valve replacement?
  → Ejection fraction under 50% + Vmax over 5 m/s + A positive exercise stress test + Upcoming other cardiac surgery
- **as-mx-09** (single, d2, 4 options) When is a supervised exercise stress test appropriate in aortic stenosis?
  → In severe stenosis that appears asymptomatic, to unmask symptoms
- **as-mx-10** (single, d2, 4 options) How do transcatheter and surgical valve replacement compare on mortality?
  → No difference in short- or long-term mortality, regardless of surgical risk
- **as-mx-11** (multi, d3, 5 options) Which complications are more common after TAVR than after surgical valve replacement?
  → Vascular complications + Need for a pacemaker + Reintervention
- **as-mx-12** (single, d2, 4 options) A 58-year-old has symptomatic severe stenosis and a life expectancy of 25 years. Which approach is first-line?
  → Surgical valve replacement
- **as-mx-13** (single, d3, 4 options, fixed order) A patient with a mechanical aortic valve also has atrial fibrillation. What is the INR goal?
  → 2.5 to 3.5
- **as-mx-14** (single, d2, 4 options) After a surgical bioprosthetic aortic valve, what is the antithrombotic plan?
  → A vitamin K antagonist for 3 to 6 months, then lifelong aspirin 75 to 100 mg
- **as-mx-15** (single, d1, 4 options) What is the preferred endocarditis prophylaxis before a dental procedure after aortic valve replacement?
  → Amoxicillin 2 g as a single dose, 30 to 60 minutes before
- **as-mx-16** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) An asymptomatic patient has severe aortic stenosis and no indication for replacement yet. How often should echo be repeated?
  → Every 6 to 12 months
