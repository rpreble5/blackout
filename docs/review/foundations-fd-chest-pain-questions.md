## Chest pain in clinic: who goes to the emergency department, and what to do for the rest (12 questions)

Source: Written for Blackout, 2026-09-13. Based on the 2021 AHA/ACC chest pain guideline and standard emergency practice. Not tied to an article; review before trusting.

- **fd-cp-01** (multi, d2, 7 options) Which features of chest pain in clinic mean an ambulance to the emergency department rather than any office work-up?
  → Ongoing pain at rest for more than 20 minutes + Haemodynamic instability + Syncope with the pain + New ECG changes
- **fd-cp-02** (single, d1, 4 options, fixed order) Within how many minutes of arrival should a patient with suspected acute coronary syndrome have a 12-lead ECG?
  → 10 minutes
- **fd-cp-03** (single, d2, 4 options) What defines typical angina?
  → Substernal discomfort, brought on by exertion or emotion, relieved by rest or nitroglycerin
- **fd-cp-04** (single, d2, 4 options) Which blood test, repeated over a few hours, rules myocardial infarction in or out?
  → High-sensitivity troponin
- **fd-cp-05** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) What dose of aspirin is given for suspected acute coronary syndrome?
  → 162 to 325 mg, chewed
- **fd-cp-06** (single, d3, 4 options) A patient with chest pain took sildenafil last night. What must be avoided?
  → Nitroglycerin, for 24 hours after sildenafil and 48 hours after tadalafil
- **fd-cp-07** (single, d3, 4 options) Tearing chest pain radiating to the back, a pulse deficit, and a widened mediastinum. Which test?
  → CT angiography of the aorta
- **fd-cp-08** (single, d2, 4 options) Sharp chest pain worse lying flat and better sitting forward, with a friction rub and diffuse ST elevation. What is the treatment?
  → An NSAID plus colchicine
- **fd-cp-09** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) A HEART score at or below what value identifies low-risk chest pain suitable for early discharge with a negative troponin?
  → 3
- **fd-cp-10** (single, d3, 4 options) Stable chest pain, intermediate pretest probability, age 55, no known coronary disease. Which first test is reasonable?
  → Coronary CT angiography
- **fd-cp-11** (multi, d2, 7 options) Which drugs are standard for a patient with confirmed stable angina?
  → Aspirin + A high-intensity statin + A beta blocker + Sublingual nitroglycerin as needed
- **fd-cp-12** (single, d2, 4 options) Chest wall tenderness reproduces the patient's pain. What does that finding do to the probability of a coronary cause?
  → Lowers it without excluding it
