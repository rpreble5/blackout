## Reflux: the empirical trial, alarm features, and when to scope (12 questions)

Source: Written for Blackout, 2026-09-13. Based on the 2022 ACG guideline on gastro-oesophageal reflux disease. Not tied to an article; review before trusting.

- **fd-gerd-01** (single, d1, 4 options, fixed order) For typical heartburn and regurgitation with no alarm features, how long is the empirical proton pump inhibitor trial?
  → 8 weeks
- **fd-gerd-02** (multi, d2, 7 options) Which alarm features mean endoscopy before, or instead of, an empirical trial?
  → Difficulty swallowing + Unintended weight loss + Gastrointestinal bleeding or iron deficiency anaemia + Persistent vomiting
- **fd-gerd-03** (single, d2, 4 options) When should a proton pump inhibitor be taken?
  → 30 to 60 minutes before a meal
- **fd-gerd-04** (single, d2, 4 options) Symptoms have not improved after 8 weeks of a proton pump inhibitor taken correctly. What is next?
  → Endoscopy, ideally off the drug for 2 to 4 weeks, with reflux monitoring if it is normal
- **fd-gerd-05** (single, d2, 4 options) Symptoms are controlled after the initial course. What happens to the proton pump inhibitor?
  → Step down to the lowest effective dose, on-demand dosing, or an H2 blocker
- **fd-gerd-06** (multi, d3, 7 options) Which associations with long-term proton pump inhibitor use have some evidence, enough to justify using the lowest effective dose?
  → Clostridioides difficile infection + Hypomagnesaemia + Vitamin B12 deficiency + Fractures
- **fd-gerd-07** (multi, d3, 8 options) Which risk factors, three or more of them with chronic reflux, prompt screening endoscopy for Barrett's oesophagus?
  → Male sex + Age over 50 + White ethnicity + Central obesity + Smoking + A first-degree relative with Barrett's or oesophageal adenocarcinoma
- **fd-gerd-08** (multi, d2, 6 options) Which lifestyle measures have evidence in reflux?
  → Weight loss in overweight patients + Raising the head of the bed + Avoiding meals within 2 to 3 hours of lying down
- **fd-gerd-09** (single, d3, 4 options) Chronic cough and hoarseness with no heartburn. What does the guideline advise about a proton pump inhibitor?
  → Do not treat empirically long term; get objective reflux testing first
- **fd-gerd-10** (single, d2, 4 options) A 30-year-old with intermittent food impaction and dysphagia. Which diagnosis must the endoscopist biopsy for?
  → Eosinophilic oesophagitis
- **fd-gerd-11** (single, d2, 4 options) Chest pain that might be reflux in a 58-year-old with risk factors. What comes first?
  → Exclude a cardiac cause
- **fd-gerd-12** (single, d3, 4 options) Who is a candidate for anti-reflux surgery?
  → Proven reflux that responds to a PPI, in a patient who cannot or will not take it long term
