## Hyperkalemia: thresholds, the ECG, and the order of treatment (12 questions)

Source: Written for Blackout, 2026-09-13. Based on standard emergency and nephrology teaching, including KDIGO and AHA guidance. Not tied to an article; review before trusting.

- **fd-hk-01** (single, d1, 4 options, fixed order) From what serum potassium is hyperkalemia usually called severe, before any ECG change?
  → 6.5 mEq/L
- **fd-hk-02** (single, d1, 4 options) What is usually the earliest ECG change of hyperkalemia?
  → Peaked T waves
- **fd-hk-03** (single, d2, 4 options) Potassium is 7.1 with a widened QRS. What is given first?
  → Intravenous calcium
- **fd-hk-04** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) What is the standard dose of intravenous regular insulin for shifting potassium into cells?
  → 10 units
- **fd-hk-05** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) For how long after intravenous insulin should glucose be monitored for hypoglycaemia?
  → 4 to 6 hours
- **fd-hk-06** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) What nebulised salbutamol dose is used to shift potassium?
  → 10 to 20 mg
- **fd-hk-07** (multi, d2, 6 options) Which treatments actually remove potassium from the body?
  → Loop diuretics + Potassium binders + Dialysis
- **fd-hk-08** (single, d2, 4 options) When does sodium bicarbonate help in hyperkalemia?
  → When there is a coexisting metabolic acidosis
- **fd-hk-09** (single, d2, 4 options) An outpatient sample shows potassium 6.2, the patient feels well, and the ECG is normal. What is done before treating?
  → Repeat the sample, checking for haemolysis and drawn without fist clenching
- **fd-hk-10** (multi, d2, 7 options) Which drugs commonly cause hyperkalemia?
  → ACE inhibitors and ARBs + Spironolactone + Trimethoprim + NSAIDs
- **fd-hk-11** (single, d3, 4 options) Which potassium binder has largely replaced sodium polystyrene sulfonate?
  → Sodium zirconium cyclosilicate or patiromer
- **fd-hk-12** (single, d2, 4 options) When is urgent dialysis the treatment for hyperkalemia?
  → Severe hyperkalemia with kidney failure or when medical treatment fails
