## Opioids: safe prescribing, conversions, and tapering (12 questions)

Source: Written for Blackout, 2026-09-13. Based on the 2022 CDC clinical practice guideline for prescribing opioids. Not tied to an article; review before trusting.

- **fd-opi-01** (single, d1, 4 options) For acute musculoskeletal pain, what is tried before an opioid?
  → Non-opioid analgesics such as NSAIDs and acetaminophen, with non-drug measures
- **fd-opi-02** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) When an opioid is needed for acute pain, what supply is usually enough?
  → 3 to 7 days
- **fd-opi-03** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) Above what daily morphine milligram equivalent does the guideline advise particular caution and reassessment?
  → 50 MME
- **fd-opi-04** (multi, d2, 6 options) Which situations call for co-prescribing naloxone?
  → 50 MME per day or more + A concurrent benzodiazepine + A history of overdose or substance use disorder
- **fd-opi-05** (single, d2, 4 options) Which combination carries the highest risk of fatal respiratory depression?
  → An opioid with a benzodiazepine
- **fd-opi-06** (single, d2, 4 options) Before prescribing a controlled substance, which database is checked?
  → The state prescription drug monitoring programme
- **fd-opi-07** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) What is the morphine milligram equivalent conversion factor for oxycodone?
  → 1.5
- **fd-opi-08** (single, d3, 4 options, fixed order) When rotating from one opioid to another, by how much is the calculated equivalent dose reduced for incomplete cross-tolerance?
  → 25% to 50%
- **fd-opi-09** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) For a patient on long-term opioids, what is a usual tapering rate?
  → About 10% of the dose per month
- **fd-opi-10** (single, d2, 4 options) Which bowel regimen is started with any regular opioid?
  → A scheduled stimulant laxative such as senna
- **fd-opi-11** (single, d3, 4 options) Which opioid is preferred for a patient with kidney failure?
  → Hydromorphone or fentanyl
- **fd-opi-12** (single, d2, 4 options) A patient on long-term opioids shows loss of control, craving and continued use despite harm. What is the treatment?
  → Buprenorphine or methadone for opioid use disorder
