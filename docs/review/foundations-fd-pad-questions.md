## Peripheral artery disease: diagnosis, claudication, and limb threat (12 questions)

Source: Written for Blackout, 2026-09-13. Based on the 2024 ACC/AHA peripheral artery disease guideline. Not tied to an article; review before trusting.

- **fd-pad-01** (single, d1, 4 options, fixed order) At or below what ankle-brachial index is peripheral artery disease diagnosed?
  → 0.90
- **fd-pad-02** (single, d2, 4 options) What is the first-line treatment for claudication?
  → A supervised exercise programme
- **fd-pad-03** (multi, d2, 6 options) Which drugs are recommended for every patient with symptomatic peripheral artery disease?
  → A high-intensity statin + A single antiplatelet agent
- **fd-pad-04** (single, d2, 4 options) Which drug improves walking distance in claudication but is contraindicated in heart failure?
  → Cilostazol
- **fd-pad-05** (multi, d2, 6 options) Which findings define chronic limb-threatening ischaemia?
  → Rest pain lasting more than two weeks + A non-healing ulcer + Gangrene
- **fd-pad-06** (single, d3, 4 options) A leg becomes suddenly painful, pale, pulseless and cold. What is the time frame for action?
  → Hours; it is a vascular emergency
- **fd-pad-07** (single, d2, 4 options) When is revascularisation offered for claudication?
  → When symptoms limit lifestyle despite exercise and medical therapy
- **fd-pad-08** (multi, d3, 7 options) Who should have an ankle-brachial index measured to screen for peripheral artery disease?
  → Adults 65 or older + Adults 50 to 64 with diabetes or a smoking history + Anyone under 50 with diabetes plus another risk factor + Anyone with known atherosclerosis elsewhere
- **fd-pad-09** (single, d2, 4 options) Which single risk factor change has the biggest effect on progression of peripheral artery disease?
  → Stopping smoking
- **fd-pad-10** (single, d2, 4 options) In a patient with diabetes and peripheral artery disease, which routine element of care prevents amputations?
  → Regular foot inspection and protective footwear
- **fd-pad-11** (single, d2, 4 options) How does claudication differ from spinal stenosis pain?
  → Claudication comes on with a predictable distance and eases with standing still
- **fd-pad-12** (single, d3, 4 options) Which added antithrombotic reduced major limb events in symptomatic peripheral artery disease?
  → Rivaroxaban 2.5 mg twice daily with aspirin
