## Pulmonary embolism: pretest probability, D-dimer, and when to image (12 questions)

Source: Written for Blackout, 2026-09-13. Based on the 2019 ESC pulmonary embolism guideline and ACP guidance on evaluation. Not tied to an article; review before trusting.

- **fd-pe-01** (multi, d2, 6 options) Which items score three points in the Wells score for pulmonary embolism?
  → Clinical signs of deep vein thrombosis + Pulmonary embolism is the most likely diagnosis
- **fd-pe-02** (single, d2, 4 options) When is a D-dimer the right test?
  → When the pretest probability is low or intermediate
- **fd-pe-03** (single, d3, 4 options) How is the D-dimer cutoff adjusted for a 70-year-old?
  → Age times 10 ng/mL, so 700
- **fd-pe-04** (multi, d3, 7 options) The PERC rule allows a low-probability patient to skip all testing when which of these are absent?
  → Age 50 or older + Heart rate 100 or more + Oxygen saturation under 95% + Oestrogen use + Previous venous thromboembolism
- **fd-pe-05** (single, d1, 4 options) What is the first-line imaging test for suspected pulmonary embolism?
  → CT pulmonary angiography
- **fd-pe-06** (single, d3, 4 options) A pregnant woman has suspected pulmonary embolism. What is a reasonable first imaging step?
  → Compression ultrasound of the legs; if negative, proceed to lung imaging
- **fd-pe-07** (single, d2, 4 options) Pulmonary embolism is likely and the CT will take an hour. What is done now?
  → Start anticoagulation while awaiting the scan
- **fd-pe-08** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) A systolic blood pressure below what, sustained for 15 minutes, defines high-risk pulmonary embolism?
  → 90 mm Hg
- **fd-pe-09** (single, d3, 4 options) A normotensive patient has right ventricular strain on CT and a raised troponin. What is the treatment?
  → Anticoagulation with close monitoring; thrombolysis only if they deteriorate
- **fd-pe-10** (multi, d3, 6 options) Which are absolute contraindications to systemic thrombolysis?
  → Any prior intracranial haemorrhage + Ischaemic stroke within three months + Active bleeding
- **fd-pe-11** (single, d2, 4 options) Which score identifies a patient with pulmonary embolism who can be treated at home?
  → The simplified PESI, at a score of zero
- **fd-pe-12** (single, d3, 4 options) A CT shows a single subsegmental embolus, leg ultrasound is negative, and the patient is well with no cancer. Which option is reasonable?
  → Clinical surveillance without anticoagulation
