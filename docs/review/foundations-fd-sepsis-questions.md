## Sepsis: recognising it and the first hour (12 questions)

Source: Written for Blackout, 2026-09-13. Based on the 2021 Surviving Sepsis Campaign guideline. Not tied to an article; review before trusting.

- **fd-sep-01** (single, d2, 4 options) How is sepsis defined?
  → Infection with organ dysfunction, a SOFA rise of 2 or more
- **fd-sep-02** (single, d2, 4 options) What does the guideline say about using qSOFA alone to screen for sepsis?
  → Not recommended as a single screening tool; it is too insensitive
- **fd-sep-03** (single, d1, 4 options, fixed order) In septic shock, within how long of recognition should antibiotics be given?
  → 1 hour
- **fd-sep-04** (single, d2, 4 options) Blood cultures have not yet been drawn and antibiotics are due. What happens?
  → Draw cultures now and give antibiotics at once; neither waits long for the other
- **fd-sep-05** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) For sepsis-induced hypotension or a lactate of 4 or more, how much crystalloid is given in the first three hours?
  → 30 mL per kg
- **fd-sep-06** (single, d2, 4 options) Which vasopressor is first line in septic shock?
  → Norepinephrine
- **fd-sep-07** (single, d1, 4 options, fixed order) What mean arterial pressure is targeted in septic shock?
  → 65 mm Hg
- **fd-sep-08** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) A lactate is 3.5 on arrival. When is it remeasured?
  → Within 2 to 4 hours
- **fd-sep-09** (single, d3, 4 options) When is hydrocortisone given in septic shock?
  → When vasopressor need persists, about norepinephrine 0.25 micrograms per kg per minute or more
- **fd-sep-10** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) Within how long should source control, such as draining an abscess or removing an infected line, be achieved?
  → 6 to 12 hours
- **fd-sep-11** (single, d2, 4 options) Cultures return with a sensitive organism after 48 hours of broad antibiotics. What happens?
  → Narrow the antibiotics to target it
- **fd-sep-12** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) Above what glucose is an insulin infusion started in a septic patient in intensive care?
  → 180 mg/dL
