## HFrEF: starting and titrating guideline-directed therapy (15 questions)

- **hf-gd-01** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) By about how much does the combination of all four GDMT classes reduce all-cause mortality compared with placebo?
  → About 61%
- **hf-gd-02** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) Within what window after diagnosis should GDMT be titrated to target doses?
  → Within 6 to 12 weeks
- **hf-gd-03** (single, d3, 4 options) A patient on lisinopril is being switched to sacubitril-valsartan. What must happen first?
  → A 36-hour washout after the last ACE inhibitor dose
- **hf-gd-04** (single, d2, 4 options) Which is the preferred first-line renin-angiotensin agent in HFrEF?
  → Sacubitril-valsartan
- **hf-gd-05** (single, d2, 4 options) When is sacubitril-valsartan best tolerated at initiation?
  → While the patient is still congested, often during an admission
- **hf-gd-06** (multi, d2, 6 options) Which beta blockers have proven mortality benefit in HFrEF?
  → Bisoprolol + Carvedilol + Metoprolol succinate
- **hf-gd-07** (single, d3, 4 options) A patient on long-term carvedilol is admitted with decompensated heart failure. What should happen to the beta blocker?
  → Continue it through the admission
- **hf-gd-08** (single, d2, 4 options) When should a beta blocker be started in newly diagnosed HFrEF?
  → Once the patient has been adequately diuresed
- **hf-gd-09** (single, d2, 4 options) Why might eplerenone be chosen over spironolactone?
  → Fewer endocrine adverse effects such as gynecomastia
- **hf-gd-10** (single, d1, 4 options) In which HFrEF patients do SGLT-2 inhibitors reduce morbidity and mortality?
  → All patients, regardless of diabetes status
- **hf-gd-11** (single, d3, 4 options, fixed order) What is the NNT for empagliflozin to prevent one cardiovascular death or heart failure hospitalisation?
  → 19
- **hf-gd-12** (multi, d3, 5 options) A newly diagnosed patient has a systolic blood pressure of 95 mm Hg. Which classes are started first?
  → A beta blocker + An SGLT-2 inhibitor
- **hf-gd-13** (single, d2, 4 options) A patient's ejection fraction has recovered from 30% to 50% on GDMT. What happens to the medications?
  → Continue all of them indefinitely
- **hf-gd-14** (single, d2, 4 options) A patient on sacubitril-valsartan has a rising BNP. How should this be interpreted?
  → Expected; follow NT-proBNP instead
- **hf-gd-15** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) How often are RAS/neprilysin inhibitors and beta blockers stepped up during titration?
  → Every 1 to 2 weeks

## HFrEF: judging volume and using diuretics (15 questions)

- **hf-cg-01** (single, d2, 4 options) Which bedside finding has the highest positive likelihood ratio for cardiogenic pulmonary oedema?
  → Jugular venous pressure over 8 cm
- **hf-cg-02** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) What is the specificity of bilateral leg oedema for cardiogenic pulmonary oedema?
  → About 10%
- **hf-cg-03** (single, d2, 4 options) Which sign is highly specific but very insensitive for pulmonary oedema?
  → Rales
- **hf-cg-04** (single, d2, 4 options) Which bedside test is more accurate than chest radiography or natriuretic peptides for acute heart failure?
  → Point-of-care ultrasound of the inferior vena cava and lungs
- **hf-cg-05** (single, d3, 4 options) On point-of-care ultrasound the inferior vena cava measures 2.5 cm and collapses less than 50% on sniff. What does this indicate?
  → Elevated right atrial pressure
- **hf-cg-06** (single, d3, 4 options) How many B-lines on lung ultrasound define interstitial oedema?
  → More than three in more than two bilateral intercostal spaces
- **hf-cg-07** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) Below what BNP level is heart failure effectively excluded?
  → 100 pg/mL
- **hf-cg-08** (multi, d3, 7 options) Which conditions raise natriuretic peptides in the absence of heart failure?
  → Pulmonary embolism + Kidney dysfunction + Tachyarrhythmias + Cirrhosis
- **hf-cg-09** (single, d1, 4 options) Which loop diuretic is preferred first line for congestion in HFrEF?
  → Furosemide
- **hf-cg-10** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) What is the usual starting dose of oral furosemide for new outpatient heart failure?
  → 20 to 40 mg daily
- **hf-cg-11** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) For a patient already on oral furosemide who is admitted with decompensation, how does the initial intravenous dose relate to the oral dose?
  → Up to twice the oral dose
- **hf-cg-12** (multi, d2, 6 options) High-dose loop diuretic is failing to decongest a patient. Which agents are appropriate to add?
  → Metolazone + Hydrochlorothiazide + Acetazolamide
- **hf-cg-13** (single, d3, 4 options, fixed order) During effective diuresis, what creatinine rise is associated with better outcomes rather than harm?
  → 0.3 to 0.5 mg/dL
- **hf-cg-14** (single, d2, 4 options, fixed order) What weight gain should prompt a patient to take an extra dose of furosemide?
  → 3 lb in a day or 5 lb in a week
- **hf-cg-15** (multi, d3, 6 options) Which findings indicate that diuresis is working?
  → Adequate urinary sodium + A falling natriuretic peptide + Haemoconcentration

- **hf-by-14** (multi, d3, 7 options) Which findings are criteria for referral to an advanced heart failure team?

- **hf-by-15** (single, d2, 4 options) What defines worsening heart failure?
  → Progressive symptoms, hospitalisation or escalating diuretic need despite stable GDMT
